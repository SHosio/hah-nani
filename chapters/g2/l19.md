# Genki II Lesson 19 (Meeting the Boss)

## Overview

This is the chapter where 敬語 arrives properly. The first two points are one
system seen from two angles. Honorific verbs raise the person you are talking
about or talking to, and お～ください turns that same machinery into the courteous
commands you hear from station staff and shop attendants. The chapter is
honorific only, so it raises the other person and never touches your own side.
Lesson 20 handles the other half, humble language that lowers you.
～てくれてありがとう thanks someone for a specific act, and half of that point is
its honorific variant くださってありがとうございました, so it stays inside the 敬語
half of the chapter. ～てよかったです expresses relief and ～はずです states what you
have good reason to believe, and those two stand on their own.

## Grammar

### Honorific Verbs
<!-- id: g2-l19-honorific-verbs -->

**What it does.** Describes the actions of someone you respect with a special
verb that bestows honor on the performer. You use honorific verbs for someone
higher up in the social hierarchy, and for someone you do not know well when you
are addressing them directly. Since the whole point is to lift the other person,
these forms never describe your own actions or those of your own group.

**Formation**

A small closed set of verbs has a dedicated honorific counterpart. Five of them
take an irregular polite form ending in います where you would expect ります.

| plain verb | honorific verb | ます-form | irregular? |
|---|---|---|---|
| いる／行く／来る | いらっしゃる | いらっしゃいます | yes |
| 見る | ご覧になる | ご覧になります | no |
| 言う | おっしゃる | おっしゃいます | yes |
| する | なさる | なさいます | yes |
| 食べる／飲む | 召し上がる | 召し上がります | no |
| くれる | くださる | くださいます | yes |
| 寝る | お休みになる | お休みになります | no |
| ～ている | ～ていらっしゃる | ～ていらっしゃいます | yes |

For everything else, the respect factor is added by rule.

| situation | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| sentence has the helping verb ている | ています → ていらっしゃいます | 話しています → 話していらっしゃいます |
| most other verbs | お + verb stem + になる | 帰りました → お帰りになりました |
| verb plus a post-predicate expression | make the verb honorific, leave the tail alone | 読んだことがあります → お読みになったことがあります |
| verb plus てください | make the verb honorific, leave ください alone | 使ってください → お使いになってください |
| verb plus ないでください | negate the honorific verb, leave ないでください alone | 無理しないでください → 無理なさらないでください |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 部長は来週出張にいらっしゃいます。 | Buchou wa raishuu shucchou ni irasshaimasu. | The department manager is going on a business trip next week. |
| 社長は毎朝コーヒーを召し上がります。 | Shachou wa maiasa koohii wo meshiagarimasu. | The president drinks coffee every morning. |
| 先生はもうこの映画をご覧になりました。 | Sensei wa mou kono eiga wo goran ni narimashita. | The teacher has already seen this film. |
| 奥様は何とおっしゃいましたか。 | Okusama wa nan to osshaimashita ka. | What did your wife say? |
| 部長は週末にゴルフをなさいます。 | Buchou wa shuumatsu ni gorufu wo nasaimasu. | The department manager plays golf at the weekend. |
| 山下先生は今、研究室で本を読んでいらっしゃいます。 | Yamashita sensei wa ima, kenkyuushitsu de hon wo yonde irasshaimasu. | Professor Yamashita is reading a book in his office right now. |
| 隣の奥様が花をくださいました。 | Tonari no okusama ga hana wo kudasaimashita. | The lady next door kindly gave me flowers. |
| 社長はもうお休みになりました。 | Shachou wa mou oyasumi ni narimashita. | The president has already gone to bed. |
| 先生は私のレポートをお読みになりましたか。 | Sensei wa watashi no repooto wo oyomi ni narimashita ka. | Has the professor read my report? |

**Notes**

- Every special honorific verb in the table above is a u-verb. ご覧になる,
  召し上がる and お休みになる end in -る and still conjugate as u-verbs, so the
  negative of ご覧になる is ご覧にならない.
- いらっしゃる covers いる, 行く and 来る all at once, so a sentence like
  先生は学校にいらっしゃいません is three-way ambiguous until context settles it.
- The five irregular polite forms are いらっしゃいます, おっしゃいます, なさいます,
  くださいます and ～ていらっしゃいます. Writing いらっしゃります is the standard slip.
- A special honorific verb outranks the ていらっしゃる rule. テレビをご覧になっています
  is the preferred shape, though 見ていらっしゃいます is also accepted.
- Post-predicate expressions such as ことがあります and ください stay as they are. The
  same applies to てもいい and てはいけない and to the potential forms, but talking
  about what an honorable person can, cannot, must or must not do is in poor
  taste to begin with, so those combinations are rare.
- Nouns and adjectives take the prefixes お or ご. お goes with native Japanese
  words (お名前, お仕事, お元気, お忙しい) and ご goes with words borrowed from
  Chinese (ご両親, ご兄弟, ご病気, ご親切).
- Some words are replaced outright. 家 becomes お宅, 子供 becomes お子さん, だれ
  becomes どなた or どちら様, どこ becomes どちら, and どうですか becomes いかがですか.
- None of this is ever aimed at yourself, your family, or the group you belong
  to. You answer ご両親はお元気ですか with おかげさまで元気です, and 元気 stays bare.
- In honorific speech the conditional たら attaches to the long form, so a shop
  attendant says お決まりになりましたら instead of 決まったら.

**Contrast**

- Lesson 20 introduces humble verbs, which lower you and your group so that the
  other person ends up higher by comparison. Lesson 19 raises them directly.
  Applying an honorific verb to your own actions is a register error, since you
  end up exalting yourself. These verbs never describe what you or your own
  group do.
- くださる is the honorific of くれる, so it keeps くれる's direction. The gift or
  favour still comes towards you.

### Giving Respectful Advice
<!-- id: g2-l19-respectful-advice -->

**What it does.** Tells someone politely to do something, in the register used by
public address announcements and store attendants. Despite ending in ください,
these sentences work as courteously phrased commands. The action is one the
listener performs for their own benefit.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| u-verb or ru-verb | お + ます-stem + ください | 取る → お取りください |
| most する compounds | ご + noun part + ください | 注意する → ご注意ください |
| 見る, 食べる／飲む, 寝る | no rule to apply, learn the three set forms | ご覧ください, お召し上がりください, お休みください |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| こちらにおかけください。 | Kochira ni okake kudasai. | Please take a seat over here. |
| どうぞお召し上がりください。 | Douzo omeshiagari kudasai. | Please help yourself. |
| 足元にご注意ください。 | Ashimoto ni gochuui kudasai. | Please watch your step. |
| こちらの写真をご覧ください。 | Kochira no shashin wo goran kudasai. | Please look at this photograph. |
| 今日はゆっくりお休みください。 | Kyou wa yukkuri oyasumi kudasai. | Please have a good rest today. |
| 三名様、こちらへどうぞお進みください。 | Sanmeisama, kochira e douzo osusumi kudasai. | Party of three, please come this way. |

**Notes**

- Because the pattern encourages the listener to act for their own good, it is
  wrong for asking a favour that benefits you. 塩をお取りください as a way of getting
  the salt passed is a mistake. Use 塩を取っていただけませんか.
- With most する compound verbs the prefix ご appears instead of お, as in
  ご注意ください and ご遠慮ください.
- The three forms tied to special honorific verbs are worth memorising as items.
  ご覧ください comes from ご覧になる, お召し上がりください from 召し上がる, and
  お休みください from お休みになる. Only 召し上がる feeds the お + stem shape
  directly. The other two drop になる and put ください straight after the noun ご覧
  or お休み.
- The pattern is everywhere in service Japanese. Announcements, elevators,
  restaurant staff and airline crew all run on it, so it is worth recognising
  even before you need to produce it.

**Contrast**

- 〜てください is the neutral everyday request and claims no social distance.
- 〜ていただけませんか is the form to reach for when you want the favour yourself.
- なさい (Lesson 22) is a command aimed downward at a child or a student, so it
  sits at the opposite end of the scale from お〜ください.

### ～てくれてありがとう
<!-- id: g2-l19-tekurete-arigatou -->

**What it does.** Thanks someone while naming the exact action you are grateful
for, instead of leaving it implicit behind a bare ありがとう.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| casual, to a peer | verb te-form + くれてありがとう | 送ってくれてありがとう |
| to a superior or someone you are not close to | verb te-form + くださってありがとうございました | 書いてくださってありがとうございました |
| thanking someone for being a certain way | ～でいてくれてありがとう | 元気でいてくれてありがとう |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 空港まで送ってくれてありがとう。 | Kuukou made okutte kurete arigatou. | Thanks for driving me to the airport. |
| ごちそうしてくれてありがとう。 | Gochisou shite kurete arigatou. | Thank you for treating me to the meal. |
| パーティーに呼んでくれてありがとう。 | Paatii ni yonde kurete arigatou. | Thank you for inviting me to the party. |
| 毎朝起こしてくれてありがとう。 | Maiasa okoshite kurete arigatou. | Thanks for waking me up every morning. |
| 駅まで来てくださってありがとうございました。 | Eki made kite kudasatte arigatou gozaimashita. | Thank you for coming all the way to the station. |
| 家に寄ってくださってありがとうございました。 | Ie ni yotte kudasatte arigatou gozaimashita. | Thank you for stopping by my house. |

**Notes**

- くださって is the honorific くださる in te-form. Genki shows the honorific version
  with ありがとうございました, where the past tense marks a favour already
  completed. ありがとうございます works just as well when the act is still going on
  or has only just started, so rank alone does not force the past.
- The plain くれてありがとう is casual, so keep it for friends and family.
- Swapping です for でいる lets you thank someone for being a certain kind of
  person rather than for doing something.
- お礼 is the noun for an expression of gratitude, useful when you talk about
  thanking rather than doing it.

**Contrast**

- くださる in this pattern is the same honorific verb from the first grammar
  point, which is where most learners meet it for the first time.
- すみません also functions as thanks in Japanese, with an added note of apology
  for the trouble caused. てくれてありがとう carries no apology at all.

### ～てよかったです
<!-- id: g2-l19-te-yokatta -->

**What it does.** Says you are glad something is or was the case. It is the
standard way to express relief about how a situation turned out.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| affirmative verb | verb te-form + よかったです | 来てよかったです |
| negative verb | ない-form → なくて + よかったです | 行かなくてよかったです |
| い-adjective | ～くて + よかったです | 天気がよくてよかったです |
| な-adjective or noun | ～で + よかったです | 元気でよかったです |
| casual | drop です | 晴れてよかった |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 日本に来てよかったです。 | Nihon ni kite yokatta desu. | I am glad I came to Japan. |
| 晴れてよかったです。 | Harete yokatta desu. | I am glad it turned out sunny. |
| 出張の前に敬語を覚えてよかったです。 | Shucchou no mae ni keigo wo oboete yokatta desu. | I am glad I learned honorific language before the business trip. |
| 授業に遅れなくてよかったです。 | Jugyou ni okurenakute yokatta desu. | I am glad I was not late for class. |
| 部長と知り合ってよかったです。 | Buchou to shiriatte yokatta desu. | I am glad I got to know the department manager. |
| 引っ越さなくてよかったです。 | Hikkosanakute yokatta desu. | I am glad I did not move. |
| 部屋が広くてよかったです。 | Heya ga hirokute yokatta desu. | I am glad the room is spacious. |
| 皆さんが元気でよかったです。 | Minasan ga genki de yokatta desu. | I am glad everyone is well. |

**Notes**

- よかった stays in the past even when the situation it comments on is still
  going on. この大学を選んでよかったです works while you are still enrolled.
- Genki prescribes なくて on the negative side, so 行かなくてよかったです is the shape
  to produce. 行かないでよかった does turn up in speech, and treating なくて as the
  strong preference keeps you on safe ground.
- Relief about something that did not happen is one of the most common uses, so
  the negative form is worth drilling on its own.
- Beyond this lesson, whose own examples are all verbs, adjectives and nouns
  have te-forms of their own and join よかった directly, with no verb in between.
  部屋が広くてよかったです, 天気がよくてよかったです and 元気でよかったです all stand as
  they are.

**Contrast**

- 〜ばよかったです is the mirror image and expresses regret about what you failed
  to do. It builds on the ば form rather than on the te-form.
- 〜てうれしいです reports an emotion in the present. 〜てよかったです evaluates an
  outcome after the fact.

### ～はずです
<!-- id: g2-l19-hazu-desu -->

**What it does.** States that something is supposed to be the case. You believe
it is true or likely, the surrounding situation and ordinary common sense point
that way, and you have no conclusive evidence either way.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | short form + はずです | 引っ越したはずです, 遅れなかったはずです |
| い-adjective | short form + はずです | 忙しいはずです |
| な-adjective | stem + な + はずです | 上手なはずです |
| noun | noun + の + はずです | 大学生のはずです |
| expectation that failed | present-tense clause + はずでした | 部長がいらっしゃるはずでした |
| inconceivable | short form + はずがありません | 遅れるはずがありません |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 山下先生は今日出張だから、大学にいらっしゃらないはずです。 | Yamashita sensei wa kyou shucchou dakara, daigaku ni irassharanai hazu desu. | Professor Yamashita is on a business trip today, so he should not be at the university. |
| 天気予報を見たから、明日は晴れるはずです。 | Tenki yohou wo mita kara, ashita wa hareru hazu desu. | I checked the forecast, so it should be sunny tomorrow. |
| 部長のお子さんはまだ中学生のはずです。 | Buchou no okosan wa mada chuugakusei no hazu desu. | The manager's child should still be in junior high school. |
| ジョンさんは今日来るはずでしたが、まだ来ていません。 | Jon-san wa kyou kuru hazu deshita ga, mada kite imasen. | John was supposed to come today, but he still has not arrived. |
| 山田さんはまじめな人だから、怒っているはずがありません。 | Yamada-san wa majime na hito dakara, okotte iru hazu ga arimasen. | Mr Yamada is a serious person, so there is no way he is angry. |
| 話はおとといもう決まったはずです。 | Hanashi wa ototoi mou kimatta hazu desu. | I am fairly sure the matter was already settled the day before yesterday. |

**Notes**

- English "supposed to" also covers duty, responsibility and law. はずです does
  not reach that far. Obligation goes through なければいけません or ないといけません.
- はずでした describes an expectation that reality then contradicted. The clause
  in front of はずでした stays in the present tense, as in 部長がいらっしゃるはずでした.
- Negation normally lives inside the clause, giving 遅れなかったはずです. The separate
  form はずがありません or はずがない makes a stronger claim, that the thing is
  inconceivable.
- Nouns take の in front of はず, giving 大学生のはずです. Watch the mismatch with
  ので (Lesson 12) and のに (Lesson 22), which take な after a noun, as in
  学生なので and 学生なのに. な-adjectives behave the same way for all three and
  take な.
- はず is a noun, so it can carry the usual sentence-final variations, including
  はずだ in casual speech and はずですね when you are seeking agreement.

**Contrast**

- でしょう is a guess and can rest on very little. はずです points at reasoning the
  listener could follow.
- 〜と思います offers your opinion and leaves plenty of room for being wrong.
  はずです is closer to a conclusion.
- 〜そうです (hearsay) attributes the information to someone else, while はずです
  keeps responsibility for the inference with you.

1. (g2-l19-honorific-verbs) Give 10 plain verbs from the honorific set and ask
   for both the honorific verb and its ます-form, mixing in the five with
   irregular います endings.
2. (g2-l19-honorific-verbs) Produce 8 sentences about a professor or a
   department manager, half using a special honorific verb and half using
   お + stem + になる, with the plain-Japanese equivalent alongside each.
3. (g2-l19-honorific-verbs) Present 6 sentences in which the speaker mistakenly
   applies an honorific verb to themselves or to their own family, and ask for
   the correction.
4. (g2-l19-honorific-verbs) Drill the お／ご prefix on 10 nouns and adjectives,
   and add the replacement words お宅, お子さん, どなた, どちら and いかがですか.
5. (g2-l19-respectful-advice) Write 8 announcements a station attendant, a shop
   clerk or a flight attendant would make using お〜ください or ご〜ください.
6. (g2-l19-respectful-advice) Show 5 requests that wrongly use お〜ください to ask
   a favour for the speaker, and rewrite each with 〜ていただけませんか.
7. (g2-l19-tekurete-arigatou) Produce 6 thank-you lines naming a specific
   action, then rewrite each for a superior with くださってありがとうございました.
8. (g2-l19-te-yokatta) Write 8 sentences expressing relief, three affirmative
   with a verb, three with the なくて negative, and two built on an adjective or
   a noun, using this chapter's words.
9. (g2-l19-hazu-desu) Build 8 はずです sentences that each state the circumstance
   first with から, covering verbs, い-adjectives, な-adjectives and nouns.
10. (g2-l19-hazu-desu) Give 5 situations of duty or obligation and 5 of reasoned
    expectation, and ask which ones はずです can express and which need
    なければいけません.
11. (g2-l19-honorific-verbs, g2-l19-respectful-advice, g2-l19-tekurete-arigatou)
    Write a short dialogue in which a student meets their professor at the
    airport, greets them, seats them, and thanks them afterwards.
12. (g2-l19-te-yokatta, g2-l19-hazu-desu) Write a short monologue by someone
    waiting for a guest who was supposed to arrive, using both patterns.
