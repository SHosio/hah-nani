# Genki II Lesson 17 (Grumble and Gossip)

## Overview

The chapter is about passing on what other people said and complaining about it,
so its first two points are both reporting devices. そうです marks a hearsay
report in polite speech and って does the same job casually. The remaining four
points are unrelated to reporting and to each other. たら is the everyday
conditional, なくてもいいです lifts an obligation, みたいです says something looks
or seems to be the case, and 前に／てから put two events in order. The one thing
to keep straight from the start is that this chapter's そうです is not the そうです
of Lesson 13. They attach to different forms and mean different things.

| point | id | one-line summary |
|---|---|---|
| ～そうです (I hear) | g2-l17-sou-desu-hearsay | Report what you heard, attached to a short form. |
| ～って | g2-l17-tte | The casual spoken version of the same report. |
| ～たら | g2-l17-tara | If and when the condition is met, the result follows. |
| ～なくてもいいです | g2-l17-nakute-mo-ii | You do not have to do it. |
| ～みたいです | g2-l17-mitai-desu | Resembles a noun, or looks like it is the case. |
| ～前に / ～てから | g2-l17-mae-ni-tekara | B before A, and A and then B. |

## Grammar

### ～そうです (I hear)
<!-- id: g2-l17-sou-desu-hearsay -->

**What it does.** Marks the whole sentence as second-hand information. You heard
it from a person, read it in print, or caught it on a broadcast, and そうです
signals that you are relaying it rather than vouching for it. The reported
sentence keeps the tense and the polarity of the original utterance, so the only
change you make is turning its predicate into a short form.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | short form + そうです | 話す → 話すそうです |
| verb, past | past short form + そうです | 離婚した → 離婚したそうです |
| verb, negative | ない form + そうです | 来ない → 来ないそうです |
| い-adjective | short form + そうです (い stays) | さびしい → さびしいそうです |
| な-adjective | stem + だ + そうです | 好きだ → 好きだそうです |
| noun + です | noun + だ + そうです | 学生だ → 学生だそうです |

The source of the information can be named in front with 〜によると.

| source phrase | reading | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| トムさんによると | Tomu-san ni yoru to | according to Tom |
| ニュースによると | nyuusu ni yoru to | according to the news |
| 天気予報によると | tenki yohou ni yoru to | according to the weather forecast |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 山田さんは離婚したそうです。 | Yamada-san wa rikon shita sou desu. | I hear that Mr Yamada got a divorce. |
| ニュースによると、あしたは雪が降るそうです。 | Nyuusu ni yoru to, ashita wa yuki ga furu sou desu. | According to the news, it will snow tomorrow. |
| あの寮は部屋がずいぶん少ないそうです。 | Ano ryou wa heya ga zuibun sukunai sou desu. | I hear that dormitory has very few rooms. |
| 木村さんは残業が嫌いだそうです。 | Kimura-san wa zangyou ga kirai da sou desu. | I hear Ms Kimura hates overtime work. |
| 隣の人はサラリーマンだそうです。 | Tonari no hito wa sarariiman da sou desu. | I hear the person next door is a company employee. |
| 田中さんは去年宝くじに当たったそうです。 | Tanaka-san wa kyonen takarakuji ni atatta sou desu. | I hear Mr Tanaka won the lottery last year. |

**Notes**

- This そうです barely conjugates. In practice you get そうです and the more casual
  そうだ and nothing else. The book rules out the negative そうじゃないです and the
  past そうでした.
- Because tense and polarity live inside the reported clause, "I heard that they
  did not have class" comes out as 授業がなかったそうです, with the past negative
  sitting before そうです.
- The だ after a な-adjective or a noun is compulsory. Dropping it produces the
  Lesson 13 pattern and changes the meaning.
- 〜によると is optional. Without it the report is still clearly second-hand, you
  simply do not say where it came from.

**Contrast**

- ～そうです in Lesson 13 means "it looks like" and attaches to stems. The two
  patterns are the most confusable pair in the book, and the attachment point is
  what separates them:

  | base | I hear that... | It looks like... |
  |---|---|---|
  | 話す | 話すそうです | (not used) |
  | さびしい | さびしいそうです | さびしそうです |
  | 好きだ | 好きだそうです | 好きそうです |
  | 学生だ | 学生だそうです | (not used) |

  A short form in front of そうです means hearsay. A bare stem in front of it
  means appearance. Nouns only work in the hearsay column.
- ～みたいです later in this chapter also renders "it looks like", but it is based
  on the speaker's own impression rather than on something somebody told them.

### ～って
<!-- id: g2-l17-tte -->

**What it does.** Does the same reporting work as そうです in informal speech. It
is the casual variant of the quotation particle と, so it either closes a
sentence as a stand-alone report or sits before a verb of saying.

**Formation**

| pattern | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| sentence-final report | short form + って | 忙しいって |
| after んです | んだ + って | 試験があるんだって |
| in place of と | quoted clause + って + 言う | 秘密だって言った |
| after ん | って shortens to て | 何と言ってた → 何て言ってた |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| あの店、いつも込むって。 | Ano mise, itsumo komu tte. | They say that shop is always crowded. |
| 山田さんは来月就職するんだって。 | Yamada-san wa raigetsu shuushoku suru n da tte. | Apparently Yamada is getting a full-time job next month. |
| 田中さんの給料、ずいぶん少ないって。 | Tanaka-san no kyuuryou, zuibun sukunai tte. | They say Tanaka's salary is really low. |
| 弟はコンタクトをするのが嫌だって言ってた。 | Otouto wa kontakuto wo suru no ga iya da tte itteta. | My little brother said he hates wearing contact lenses. |
| 秘密だって言ったでしょう。 | Himitsu da tte itta deshou. | I told you it was a secret, didn't I. |
| 山下先生は寮に住んでるって聞いた。 | Yamashita-sensei wa ryou ni sunderu tte kiita. | I heard that Professor Yamashita lives in a dorm. |

**Notes**

- って follows short forms the way 〜と言っていました and そうです do, so the same
  だ appears after な-adjectives and nouns.
- If you want to quote somebody word for word, って and と can also follow a long
  form and even keep sentence-final particles like か, ね and よ. That preserves
  the style and tone of the original speaker.
- って after ん contracts to て, which is why the everyday question is 何て言ってた
  rather than 何って言ってた.
- This is spoken register. In writing or in a polite conversation, use そうです
  or 〜と言っていました instead.

**Contrast**

- そうです is the polite equivalent of sentence-final って. 込むそうです and 込むって
  report the same thing at two different levels of formality.
- と marks a quotation neutrally in any register. って is と with the formality
  removed.

### ～たら
<!-- id: g2-l17-tara -->

**What it does.** States a condition. In A たら B, the event or situation B is
realised if and when A comes true. Depending on how likely A is, the same
sentence reads as "if A", as "when A", or as a purely hypothetical "if A were
the case".

**Formation**

The た of たら comes from the past short form, so the rule is simply past short
form plus ら.

| input | affirmative | negative |
|---|---|---|
| verb | 読む → 読んだら | 読まなかったら |
| い-adjective | やさしい → やさしかったら | やさしくなかったら |
| な-adjective | 静かだ → 静かだったら | 静かじゃなかったら |
| noun + です | 休みだ → 休みだったら | 休みじゃなかったら |

Three readings share this one form.

| reading | what A is like | example |
|---|---|---|
| possible condition | may or may not happen | 宝くじに当たったら、旅行します |
| temporal sequence | very likely to happen | 寮に帰ったら、電話します |
| contrary to fact | not the case now | 私が猫だったら、一日中寝ています |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 宝くじに当たったら、旅行会社に行きます。 | Takarakuji ni atattara, ryokou gaisha ni ikimasu. | If I win the lottery, I will go to a travel agency. |
| 寮に帰ったら、お湯を沸かします。 | Ryou ni kaettara, oyu wo wakashimasu. | When I get back to the dorm, I will boil some water. |
| 給料が少なかったら、困ります。 | Kyuuryou ga sukunakattara, komarimasu. | If the salary is low, I will be in trouble. |
| 残業がなかったら、うれしいんですけど。 | Zangyou ga nakattara, ureshii n desu kedo. | I would be glad if there were no overtime. |
| 私が男だったら、毎朝ひげをそるでしょう。 | Watashi ga otoko dattara, maiasa hige wo soru deshou. | If I were a man, I would probably shave every morning. |
| 電車が込んでいたら、次のに乗ります。 | Densha ga konde itara, tsugi no ni norimasu. | If the train is crowded, I will take the next one. |

**Notes**

- Time runs forwards through the sentence. B can only happen at the moment A
  comes true or later, so a たら sentence cannot put B earlier than A. 来週試験が
  あったら、今度の週末は勉強したほうがいいですよ is wrong, because the weekend comes
  before the exam.
- The dependency between A and B has to be a natural one. たら cannot express
  "B even if A", where B holds in spite of A. 結婚したかったら、私は結婚しません does
  not mean "I will not marry you even if you want to".
- Whether a given sentence reads as "if" or as "when" is not in the grammar at
  all. It comes from how likely A actually is. 今晩うちに帰ったら、電話します is
  "when I get home" if you expect to be home and "if I get home" if you do not.
- The already familiar 〜たらどうですか from Lesson 14 is built on this same form.

**Contrast**

- 〜ば (Lesson 22) states a general condition and blocks commands, requests and
  invitations in the main clause after an affirmative action verb. たら has no
  such restriction, which makes it the safer everyday choice.
- 〜と (Lesson 18) says the result follows automatically, so it cannot carry a
  main clause that involves anybody's decision.

### ～なくてもいいです
<!-- id: g2-l17-nakute-mo-ii -->

**What it does.** Says that something is not required. It is the release from an
obligation rather than a prohibition, so it tells the listener they are free to
skip the action, not that they must avoid it.

**Formation**

| step | operation | example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | put the predicate in the negative short form | 脱ぐ → 脱がない |
| 2 | drop the final い of ない | 脱がな |
| 3 | add くてもいいです | 脱がなくてもいいです |

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | ない → なくてもいいです | 行かない → 行かなくてもいいです |
| い-adjective | くない → くなくてもいいです | 高くない → 高くなくてもいいです |
| な-adjective | じゃない → じゃなくてもいいです | きれいじゃない → きれいじゃなくてもいいです |
| noun + です | じゃない → じゃなくてもいいです | 学生じゃない → 学生じゃなくてもいいです |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 部屋にかぎをかけなくてもいいです。 | Heya ni kagi wo kakenakute mo ii desu. | You do not have to lock the room. |
| あしたは化粧しなくてもいいです。 | Ashita wa keshou shinakute mo ii desu. | You do not need to put on makeup tomorrow. |
| ひげをそらなくてもいいです。 | Hige wo soranakute mo ii desu. | You do not need to shave. |
| 紙は新しくなくてもいいです。 | Kami wa atarashikunakute mo ii desu. | The paper does not have to be new. |
| 部屋はきれいじゃなくてもいいです。 | Heya wa kirei ja nakute mo ii desu. | The room does not have to be tidy. |
| コンタクトをしなくてもいいですか。 | Kontakuto wo shinakute mo ii desu ka. | Is it all right if I do not wear contact lenses? |

**Notes**

- なくて is the negative te-form introduced in Lesson 16, so this pattern is the
  negative counterpart of 〜てもいいです.
- The も can be dropped. 脱がなくていいです is the same thing slightly more casually.
- Not required is not the same as forbidden. To say the action must not happen,
  you need 〜てはいけません.
- The pattern applies to adjectives and nouns as readily as to verbs, which is
  how you set a lower bound on a requirement, as in 高くなくてもいいです.

**Contrast**

- 〜なければいけません states the obligation this pattern removes. The two are the
  standard drill pair.
- 〜なくてもいいです grants permission to omit. 〜ないでください asks the listener to
  refrain, which is the opposite instruction.

### ～みたいです
<!-- id: g2-l17-mitai-desu -->

**What it does.** Two related jobs. After a noun it says that something or
somebody resembles the thing named, usually in outward characteristics. After a
verb it says that something appears to be the case, as a guess the speaker is
drawing from what they can observe.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| noun | noun + みたいです | ゴリラみたいです |
| verb | short form + みたいです | 降る → 降るみたいです |
| verb, past | past short form + みたいです | 降った → 降ったみたいです |
| verb, negative | ない form + みたいです | 寝ない → 寝ないみたいです |
| verb, past negative | なかった form + みたいです | 寝なかった → 寝なかったみたいです |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 私の父はサンタクロースみたいです。 | Watashi no chichi wa Santa Kuroosu mitai desu. | My father is like Santa Claus. |
| あの人はストーブみたいですね。 | Ano hito wa sutoobu mitai desu ne. | That person is like a heater, isn't he. |
| お客さんはもう帰ったみたいです。 | Okyaku-san wa mou kaetta mitai desu. | It looks like the guest has already gone home. |
| 上の階の人は離婚したみたいです。 | Ue no kai no hito wa rikon shita mitai desu. | It looks like the person upstairs got a divorce. |
| 田中さんはこの寮に慣れていないみたいです。 | Tanaka-san wa kono ryou ni narete inai mitai desu. | It looks like Mr Tanaka has not got used to this dormitory. |
| かぎをかけなかったみたいです。 | Kagi wo kakenakatta mitai desu. | It looks like I did not lock up. |

**Notes**

- The noun attaches directly. There is no の and no だ in front of みたいです.
- You can use みたいです about yourself when you are not clear about your own
  situation, as in 財布を忘れたみたいです for "it looks like I left my wallet at home".
- All four tense and polarity combinations work with verbs, so the guess can be
  about the past and about things that did not happen.
- みたいです can follow adjectives too, but with adjectives そうです from Lesson 13
  is far more common, so the adjective slot is best left to そうです.

**Contrast**

- ～そうです in this chapter reports what somebody told you. みたいです reports what
  you yourself concluded from the evidence in front of you.
- ～そうです in Lesson 13 also means "it looks like", and with adjectives it is the
  form actually used. ゴリラそうです is not Japanese, ゴリラみたいです is.
- 〜のような／〜のように (Lesson 22) is the more formal way to say the noun-based
  resemblance. みたいな and みたいに are its casual equivalents.

### ～前に / ～てから
<!-- id: g2-l17-mae-ni-tekara -->

**What it does.** Puts two events in order in a single sentence. 前に names the
event that comes second and says the other one happens before it. てから names
the event that comes first and says the other one follows.

**Formation**

| pattern | shape | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 前に | verb A (short present) + 前に, verb B | B before A |
| てから | verb A (te-form) + から, verb B | A, and then B |

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| 前に | always the present short form, never the past | 帰る前に |
| 前に with a する verb | dictionary form + 前に | 就職する前に |
| てから | te-form + から | 沸かす → 沸かしてから |
| てから with 来る | 来て + から | 来る → 来てから |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 寮を出る前に、かぎをかけてください。 | Ryou wo deru mae ni, kagi wo kakete kudasai. | Please lock up before you leave the dorm. |
| 就職する前に、長い旅行がしたいです。 | Shuushoku suru mae ni, nagai ryokou ga shitai desu. | I want to take a long trip before I start a full-time job. |
| 化粧する前に、髪をとかします。 | Keshou suru mae ni, kami wo tokashimasu. | I comb my hair before I put on makeup. |
| お湯を沸かしてから、お茶をいれます。 | Oyu wo wakashite kara, ocha wo iremasu. | I boil water and then make tea. |
| 靴を脱いでから、部屋に入ります。 | Kutsu wo nuide kara, heya ni hairimasu. | I take my shoes off and then go into the room. |
| 残業が終わってから、うちに帰りました。 | Zangyou ga owatte kara, uchi ni kaerimashita. | I went home after the overtime finished. |
| この寮に住んでから、ずいぶん日本語が上手になりました。 | Kono ryou ni sunde kara, zuibun nihongo ga jouzu ni narimashita. | My Japanese has got much better since I started living in this dorm. |

**Notes**

- The verb in front of 前に is in the present tense no matter what tense the
  overall sentence is in. 日本に来る前に、日本語を勉強しました keeps 来る even though the
  whole sentence is past.
- Tense is carried by the main clause at the end of the sentence, so that is the
  only place you mark past.
- An A てから B sentence can also describe a state of B that has held ever since
  event A, which is why 猫が死んでから、とてもさびしいです means "I have been lonely
  since my cat died".
- てから is stronger than a plain te-form chain. It insists that A finished first,
  where 沸かして、お茶をいれます just lists the two actions in order.

**Contrast**

- 〜たら also sequences events, but it makes B depend on A being realised. てから
  simply reports the order of two events that both happen.
- 〜てから and 〜たあとで overlap. あとで is a neutral "after", while てから puts more
  weight on A having to be finished before B can start.

   Lesson 13 そうです and the Lesson 17 そうです, and ask which is hearsay and which
   is appearance.

   at least two that need んだって.

   reported line uses って rather than と言っていました.
6. (g2-l17-tara) Provide 10 verbs, adjectives and nouns and ask for the たら form,
   affirmative and negative.
7. (g2-l17-tara) Write 9 sentences, three each for the possible-condition,
   temporal-sequence and contrary-to-fact readings, and label which is which.
8. (g2-l17-tara) Present 6 conditional sentences, two of which put the second
   event before the first, and ask which are ungrammatical and why.
9. (g2-l17-nakute-mo-ii) Turn 8 〜なければいけません obligations into
   〜なくてもいいです releases, using this chapter's grooming and household verbs.

    than a verb carries the なくてもいいです, such as requirements on a gift or a room.
11. (g2-l17-mitai-desu) Build 6 noun-based resemblance sentences describing
    people, then 6 verb-based sentences guessing what has happened in a dorm.
12. (g2-l17-mitai-desu) Give 8 observations and ask for a みたいです conclusion,
    covering present, past, negative and past negative.

    sentence and a てから sentence for each pair.

    the verb before 前に stayed in the present.
15. (g2-l17-sou-desu-hearsay, g2-l17-tte, g2-l17-tara, g2-l17-mitai-desu) Write a
    short complaint monologue by an overworked office worker that uses all four
    patterns at least once.
