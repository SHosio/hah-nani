# Genki II Lesson 16 (Lost and Found)

## Overview

This chapter is about favours, the asking for them and the owing of them. Lesson
14 introduced あげる, くれる and もらう as verbs for handing over objects, and the
first point here turns them into helping verbs so that whole actions can be given
and received. Most of the rest of the chapter grows out of that social setting.
ていただけませんか is the polite way to ask for a favour, といい is what you say when
you are hoping things go well for somebody, and てすみませんでした is the apology for
when they did not. 時 is the odd one out, a temporal clause that attaches one
sentence to another.

| point | id | one-line summary |
|---|---|---|
| ～てあげる／～てくれる／～てもらう | g2-l16-teageru-tekureru-temorau | Doing something for somebody, and having it done for you. |

| ～てすみませんでした | g2-l16-te-sumimasen-deshita | I am sorry for having done that. |

## Grammar

### ～てあげる／～てくれる／～てもらう
<!-- id: g2-l16-teageru-tekureru-temorau -->

**What it does.** Adds the idea of a favour to an action. Plain 手伝いました simply
reports that somebody helped. 手伝ってくれました says that they helped me and that I
am grateful for it. The three helping verbs differ in the direction the favour
travels and in who ends up as the subject of the sentence, and getting those two
things straight is most of the work in this chapter.

Direction is the first thing to settle. あげる points away from the speaker. It
covers the speaker doing something for the listener, the speaker or the listener
doing something for a third party, and one third party doing something for
another. くれる points inward, towards the speaker or the speaker's side, and the
person you are talking to counts as inward as well, so a third party doing a
favour for the listener takes くれる. もらう describes the same inward-flowing
favour as くれる and repackages it with the receiver as the subject.

**Formation**

| helping verb | who is the subject | who receives the favour | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| te-form + あげる | the one doing the favour | the listener when the speaker acts, otherwise a third party | 貸してあげる |
| te-form + くれる | the one doing the favour | the speaker, the speaker's side, or the listener | 貸してくれる |
| te-form + もらう | the one receiving the favour | the subject itself | 貸してもらう |

Particles depend on which of the three you use.

| pattern | frame | particle rule |
|---|---|---|
| あげる | X は Y に／を ... てあげる | the beneficiary keeps whatever particle the main verb calls for, に for 貸す, を for 連れていく |
| くれる | X が ... てくれる | the beneficiary is understood to be the speaker and is usually left out |
| もらう | X は Y に ... てもらう | the person who performs the action is always marked に |

When the main verb has no slot for a beneficiary at all, 掃除する and 探す being
standard cases, use ～のために instead.

| gloss | japanese |
|---|---|
| The station attendant looked for my lost property for me. | 駅員さんが私のために忘れ物を探してくれました。 |
| I did the ironing for my little brother. | 私は弟のためにアイロンをかけてあげました。 |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 私は妹に漢字を教えてあげました。 | Watashi wa imouto ni kanji wo oshiete agemashita. | I taught my little sister the kanji as a favour to her. |
| 山田さんは道に迷っていた人を駅まで連れていってあげました。 | Yamada-san wa michi ni mayotte ita hito wo eki made tsurete itte agemashita. | Ms Yamada kindly took somebody who had lost their way to the station. |
| 母が毎朝私を起こしてくれます。 | Haha ga maiasa watashi wo okoshite kuremasu. | My mother wakes me up every morning. |
| 駅員さんが道を教えてくれました。 | Ekiin-san ga michi wo oshiete kuremashita. | The station attendant told me the way. |
| 友だちが晩ご飯をおごってくれました。 | Tomodachi ga bangohan wo ogotte kuremashita. | A friend treated me to dinner. |
| 私は友だちにレポートを直してもらいました。 | Watashi wa tomodachi ni repooto wo naoshite moraimashita. | I got a friend to fix my report. |
| 先生に推薦状を書いていただきました。 | Sensei ni suisenjou wo kaite itadakimashita. | I had my teacher write me a letter of recommendation. |
| 親せきが私のために奨学金の書類を集めてくれました。 | Shinseki ga watashi no tame ni shougakukin no shorui wo atsumete kuremashita. | A relative gathered the scholarship paperwork for me. |
| ホストファミリーが京都を案内してくれました。 | Hosuto famirii ga Kyouto wo annai shite kuremashita. | My host family showed me around Kyoto. |

**Notes**

- くれる and もらう can describe one and the same event.
  妹がごみを出してくれました and 私は妹にごみを出してもらいました report the same errand. What
  changes is which side of the transaction the sentence is built around.
- もらう covers a range from arranging something deliberately to simply
  acknowledging that somebody was kind. 隣の人に道を教えてもらいました works even when
  you never asked for the help.
- くれる also covers a favour done for the person you are talking to. Say
  親せきが手伝ってくれたんですね when a relative helped your listener, since あげる would be
  wrong there.
- The beneficiary in a くれる sentence is grammatically invisible most of the time,
  since everybody knows it is the speaker. Spell it out only when the sentence
  would otherwise be unclear, and then keep the main verb's own particle, as in
  友だちが私にファイルを見せてくれました, where 見せる is what calls for に.
- With somebody above you, the もらう slot takes the humble いただく and the くれる
  slot takes the honorific くださる. Lesson 16 introduces both verbs when it derives
  ～ていただけませんか and ～てくださいませんか, so 先生に推薦状を書いていただきました and
  先生が推薦状を書いてくださいました are the versions to use with a teacher.
- Beyond this lesson, あげる draws attention to the fact that you went out of your
  way, so aiming it at a social superior sounds like you are fishing for thanks.
  The way out is to drop the giving verb altogether. 荷物を持ちましょうか or the humble
  お持ちします carries the offer, while ～てさしあげる only makes the condescension
  louder.
- Requests built on these verbs make up the next grammar point, so ～てくれませんか and
  ～てもらえませんか are worth learning alongside the plain forms.
- おごる belongs among friends. ごちそうする is the polite alternative, and it is the
  one to reach for when the person treating you is a superior such as a teacher
  or a business associate, as in この間、先生にごちそうしていただきました. ごちそうする covers
  inviting somebody out for a meal as well as paying for one, as in
  親せきをレストランに呼んで、ごちそうしました.

**Contrast**

- Lesson 14's あげる／くれる／もらう transfer objects. These forms transfer actions, and
  the direction rules carry over unchanged.
- Without a helping verb the sentence is a neutral report. 友だちに砂糖を貸しました
  states a fact, and 友だちに砂糖を貸してあげました frames the same act as a kindness.

### ～ていただけませんか
<!-- id: g2-l16-te-itadakemasen-ka -->

**What it does.** Asks somebody to do something for you. The chapter gives three
levels so you can match the request to the person, running from a stranger or a
superior down to a close friend.

**Formation**

| level | pattern | who you say it to |
|---|---|---|
| polite | te-form + いただけませんか | a nonpeer or a stranger, more polite than ください |
| neutral | te-form + くれませんか | roughly as polite as ください, good in a host-family setting |
| casual | te-form + くれない？, or the bare te-form | peers and friends |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| すみません、道を教えていただけませんか。 | Sumimasen, michi wo oshiete itadakemasen ka. | Excuse me, could you tell me the way? |
| 推薦状を書いていただけませんか。 | Suisenjou wo kaite itadakemasen ka. | Would you be willing to write me a letter of recommendation? |
| この英語の手紙を訳していただけませんか。 | Kono eigo no tegami wo yakushite itadakemasen ka. | Could I ask you to translate this English letter? |
| ちょっとこのファイルを見せてくれませんか。 | Chotto kono fairu wo misete kuremasen ka. | Could you show me this file for a second? |
| 駅まで迎えに来てくれませんか。 | Eki made mukae ni kite kuremasen ka. | Would you come pick me up at the station? |
| そのファイル、取ってくれない？ | Sono fairu, totte kurenai? | Pass me that file, would you? |

**Notes**

- いただけませんか is the potential form of いただく, the humble verb for receiving
  from somebody above you, so the request literally asks whether you might be
  allowed to receive the favour. That indirectness is what makes it polite.
- くださいませんか comes from くださる, the verb for somebody above you giving to you.
  The familiar ください is a historical truncation of くださいませんか, which is why the
  two feel related.
- The book lists the full ladder in decreasing politeness. ～ていただけませんか,
  ～てくださいませんか, ～てもらえませんか, ～てくれませんか, ～てもらえない？, ～てくれない？.
- Every rung works in the affirmative too, so ～ていただけますか is available alongside
  ～ていただけませんか.
- ちょっと and すみません soften the opening, and 失礼します is the phrase for
  interrupting somebody before you ask.

**Contrast**

- ～てください from Genki I is an instruction as much as a request and sits at about
  the same level as ～てくれませんか. Reach for いただけませんか when you have no standing to
  instruct the person.
- ～てもいいですか asks permission for something you want to do yourself, where these
  forms ask somebody else to act.

### ～といい
<!-- id: g2-l16-to-ii -->

**What it does.** Expresses a hope that something good will happen. The ending
changes with whose luck is at stake. ですね and ね wish well for somebody else,
while んですが and んだけど voice a hope of your own in a modest, tentative way.

**Formation**

| input | ending | reading |
|---|---|---|
| clause in the short present form + と | いいですね／いいね | I hope it happens, said about somebody else's luck |
| clause in the short present form + と | いいんですが／いいんだけど | I hope it happens, said about your own luck |

Whose luck is at stake is the only thing the ending tracks. The clause in front
of と stays in the short present form whatever the tense of the surrounding
conversation, and it can be affirmative or negative. A negative sits inside the
clause and leaves the ending untouched, so 雨が降らないといいね hopes on your
listener's behalf and 電車に乗り遅れないといいんですが hopes on your own.

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 台風が来ないといいですね。 | Taifuu ga konai to ii desu ne. | I hope the typhoon does not come. |
| 忘れ物が出てくるといいですね。 | Wasuremono ga dete kuru to ii desu ne. | I hope the thing you lost turns up. |
| 奨学金がもらえるといいんですが。 | Shougakukin ga moraeru to ii n desu ga. | I am hoping I can get the scholarship. |
| 週末に親せきに会えるといいんだけど。 | Shuumatsu ni shinseki ni aeru to ii n da kedo. | I hope I can see my relatives at the weekend. |
| 電車に乗り遅れないといいんですが。 | Densha ni noriokurenai to ii n desu ga. | I hope I do not miss the train. |
| 明日いい天気だといいですね。 | Ashita ii tenki da to ii desu ne. | I hope the weather is good tomorrow. |

**Notes**

- The pattern is about something happening, so it does not fit an action under
  your own control. The book's advice is that you can usually turn the verb into
  the potential form to repair such a sentence. 大学院に入れるといいんですが is right,
  and 大学院に入るといいんですが is wrong.
- んですが and んだけど leave the sentence hanging, which is the whole point. The
  unfinished feel is what makes the hope sound modest.
- To hope that somebody does something for you, which amounts to an indirect
  request, put くれる in front of といい. 先生が推薦状を書いてくれるといいんですが is a gentle
  way of saying you are counting on the teacher.

**Contrast**

- ～たらどうですか from Lesson 14 offers the listener advice about what to do. This
  pattern states a wish and asks nothing of the listener.
- ～たいです is a want you intend to act on, where といい is a hope about
  circumstances outside your control.

### ～時
<!-- id: g2-l16-toki -->

**What it does.** Builds a "when A, B" sentence. Clause A supplies the time
reference and clause B carries the main event. The tricky part is the tense
inside A, which follows the order of A against B. When the whole story happened
has no bearing on it.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | short form + 時 | 行く時／行った時 |
| い-adjective | plain form + 時 | さびしい時 |
| な-adjective | stem + な + 時 | 元気な時 |
| noun | noun + の + 時 | 子供の時 |

Choosing the tense in clause A:

| situation at the moment B happens | tense in A | example |
|---|---|---|
| A is still current or still ahead | short present | 旅行に行く時、ガイドブックを買いました。 |
| A is already finished | short past | 旅行に行った時、お土産を買いました。 |
| A is an ongoing action wrapped around B | ～ている + 時 | ご飯を食べている時、地震がありました。 |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 日本に行く時、目覚まし時計を買います。 | Nihon ni iku toki, mezamashi dokei wo kaimasu. | I will buy an alarm clock before I go to Japan. |
| 日本に行った時、親せきに会いました。 | Nihon ni itta toki, shinseki ni aimashita. | I met my relatives when I was in Japan. |
| 道に迷った時、駅員さんに聞きました。 | Michi ni mayotta toki, ekiin-san ni kikimashita. | I asked a station attendant when I got lost. |
| 落ち込んだ時、親に電話します。 | Ochikonda toki, oya ni denwa shimasu. | I call my parents when I feel down. |
| 忙しい時、部屋が汚くなります。 | Isogashii toki, heya ga kitanaku narimasu. | My room gets dirty when I am busy. |
| 暇な時、部屋のごみを出します。 | Hima na toki, heya no gomi wo dashimasu. | I take the rubbish out when I have free time. |
| 子供の時、よく笑いました。 | Kodomo no toki, yoku waraimashita. | I laughed a lot when I was a child. |
| 本を読んでいる時、親せきが来ました。 | Hon wo yonde iru toki, shinseki ga kimashita. | My relatives arrived while I was reading a book. |
| 台風の時、電車が止まりました。 | Taifuu no toki, densha ga tomarimashita. | The trains stopped during the typhoon. |

**Notes**

- Run the thought experiment the book suggests. Stand at the moment of B and ask
  whether A has happened yet. 旅行に行く時 puts you before departure, so the buying
  happens at home. 旅行に行った時 puts you already at the destination.
- Clause A takes the present tense even when both events are firmly in the past.
  日本に留学する時、奨学金の書類を出しました is correct, because the paperwork went in before
  the departure.
- な-adjectives take な and nouns take の in front of 時, which is the same rule as
  any other noun modification. An い-adjective goes in as it is, as in 忙しい時.
- For an action stretched around B, use the ている form. The result-state reading of
  ている does not work here, so 国に帰っている時、目覚まし時計を買いました is wrong. 帰っている
  describes being back home already rather than the journey, and Lesson 7 is
  where the book covers that reading of ている.
- The book warns in a footnote that 時 clauses have more twists and quirks than
  these rules show, so treat the tense test as a good start rather than the whole
  story.
- Beyond this lesson, a 時 clause is a noun phrase and takes particles like any
  other noun, so 時に, 時は and 時から all turn up. Every example in Lesson 16 leaves
  時 bare.
- Lesson 16's Expression Notes cover a separate adjective point. Replace the final
  い of an い-adjective with さ to get a noun, so 大きい gives 大きさ, やさしい gives
  やさしさ and さびしい gives さびしさ. Some な-adjectives work the same way with な
  replaced by さ, as in 便利さ.

**Contrast**

- ～てから orders two events strictly, where 時 sets a time frame and lets the
  events overlap.
- ～たら from Lesson 17 is conditional and carries an "if" flavour. 時 asserts that
  the situation does occur.

### ～てすみませんでした
<!-- id: g2-l16-te-sumimasen-deshita -->

**What it does.** Apologises for something you did. The te-form of the verb names
the offence and the apology follows it. There is a matching negative form for
apologising about something you failed to do.

**Formation**

| what you are apologising for | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| something you did | te-form + すみませんでした | 遅れて、すみませんでした |
| something you did not do | short negative ない → なくて + すみませんでした | 出さなくて、すみませんでした |
| casual, either polarity | same clause + ごめん | 忘れて、ごめん |

To build the negative form, take the short negative ～ない and swap the final い
for くて.

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 朝寝坊して、すみませんでした。 | Asanebou shite, sumimasen deshita. | I am sorry for oversleeping. |
| 授業中に電話に出て、すみませんでした。 | Jugyouchuu ni denwa ni dete, sumimasen deshita. | I am sorry for taking a call during class. |
| 部屋を汚くして、すみませんでした。 | Heya wo kitanaku shite, sumimasen deshita. | I am sorry for making the room dirty. |
| 宿題を今日中に出さなくて、すみませんでした。 | Shukudai wo kyoujuu ni dasanakute, sumimasen deshita. | I am sorry for not handing the homework in by the end of today. |
| 何も説明しなくて、すみませんでした。 | Nani mo setsumei shinakute, sumimasen deshita. | I am sorry for not explaining anything. |
| 迎えに行かなくて、ごめん。 | Mukae ni ikanakute, gomen. | Sorry I did not come to pick you up. |

**Notes**

- The book builds the pattern on the te-form of a verb, and every example it gives
  names an action. Beyond this lesson the same slot accepts the te-form of an
  adjective, as in 遅くて、すみませんでした.
- Beyond this lesson, the join here is the general "cause and effect" te-form that
  links a reason to what follows from it. The apology is the consequence of the
  clause in front of it.
- ごめん is for friends and family only. Use すみませんでした with a teacher, a
  stranger or anybody at work.
- しまう pairs naturally with this pattern when the act was accidental, as in
  寝てしまって、すみませんでした.

**Contrast**

- 失礼します is said as you interrupt or leave, where てすみませんでした looks back at
  something already done.
- ありがとうございました thanks somebody for a favour received. Japanese often uses
  すみません for that as well, and the difference is whether you frame the favour as
  a kindness or as a burden you imposed.
