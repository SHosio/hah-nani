# Genki II Lesson 18 (John's Part-time Job)

## Overview

The chapter is set in a workplace where things get switched on, spilled, broken
and apologised for, and the grammar follows that setting. Transitivity pairs let
you say whether a person did something to an object or whether the object simply
changed, and てしまう adds the colour of an action carried to its end or an
accident you wish had not happened. The remaining three points are independent
of that theme. と states an automatic consequence, ながら stacks two of your own
actions onto one moment, and ばよかったです names the choice you should have made.

| point | id | one-line summary |
|---|---|---|
| Transitivity Pairs | g2-l18-transitivity-pairs | Paired verbs for doing something to a thing and for the thing changing. |
| ～てしまう | g2-l18-teshimau | Finish something off, or do it by accident and regret it. |
| ～と | g2-l18-to | Whenever A happens, B follows automatically. |
| ～ながら | g2-l18-nagara | Do two things at once, both by the same person. |
| ～ばよかったです | g2-l18-ba-yokatta | I wish I had done it, I should have done it. |

## Grammar

### Transitivity Pairs
<!-- id: g2-l18-transitivity-pairs -->

**What it does.** Splits verbs into two kinds. A transitive verb describes a
person acting on a thing, so the sentence needs both an agent and an object. An
intransitive verb describes a change the thing goes through by itself, so the
sentence needs only the thing. Most Japanese verbs sit on one side or the other
with no counterpart, and this lesson collects the important ones that come in
matched pairs.

**Formation**

The pairs themselves have to be memorised. There is a rough family resemblance
inside each row, and no rule that predicts the whole set.

| transitive | reading | does what | intransitive | reading | does what |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 開ける | akeru | you open it | 開く | aku | it opens |
| 閉める | shimeru | you close it | 閉まる | shimaru | it closes |
| 入れる | ireru | you put it in | 入る | hairu | it goes in |
| 出す | dasu | you take it out | 出る | deru | it goes out |
| つける | tsukeru | you switch it on | つく | tsuku | it comes on |
| 消す | kesu | you switch it off, you put it out | 消える | kieru | it goes off |
| 壊す | kowasu | you break it | 壊れる | kowareru | it breaks |
| 汚す | yogosu | you make it dirty | 汚れる | yogoreru | it gets dirty |
| 落とす | otosu | you drop it | 落ちる | ochiru | it falls |
| 沸かす | wakasu | you boil the water | 沸く | waku | the water boils |

The particle consequence is what the pairs are really for.

| verb type | pattern | reading |
|---|---|---|
| transitive | person が／は + thing を + verb | somebody did it to the thing |
| intransitive | thing が + verb | the thing changed |

With ている the two members drift apart as well.

| verb type | + ている | reading |
|---|---|---|
| transitive | 開けています | the action is in progress |
| intransitive | 開いています | the resulting state holds now |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 私がエアコンをつけました。 | Watashi ga eakon wo tsukemashita. | I turned the air conditioner on. |
| エアコンがつきました。 | Eakon ga tsukimashita. | The air conditioner came on. |
| ルームメイトがカーテンを開けています。 | Ruumumeito ga kaaten wo akete imasu. | My roommate is opening the curtains. |
| カーテンが開いています。 | Kaaten ga aite imasu. | The curtains are open. |
| 弟が冷蔵庫を壊しました。 | Otouto ga reizouko wo kowashimashita. | My little brother broke the refrigerator. |
| 冷蔵庫が壊れています。 | Reizouko ga kowarete imasu. | The refrigerator is broken. |
| ろうそくを消してください。 | Rousoku wo keshite kudasai. | Please put the candle out. |
| まだお湯が沸いていません。 | Mada oyu ga waite imasen. | The water has not boiled yet. |

**Notes**

- An intransitive verb never takes を for the thing that changes. ドアを開きます
  is wrong, and ドアが開きます is what you want.
- The ている split follows the older rule from Lesson 7 about activity verbs
  versus change verbs. Transitive members are activities, so ている gives an
  action in progress. Intransitive members are changes, so ている gives the state
  left behind.
- Genki marks the pattern in its own word lists with 〜を for the transitive
  members and 〜が for the intransitive ones, which is worth copying into your
  own notes.
- 開く in this lesson is あく, the intransitive partner of 開ける. The same kanji
  read ひらく is a different verb.
- English hides the distinction because many English verbs cover both sides.
  "The shop opens at nine" and "I open the shop at nine" use one verb in
  English and two in Japanese.

**Contrast**

- ～てしまう can attach to either member of a pair, and the choice still matters.
  壊してしまいました admits you broke it. 壊れてしまいました reports that it broke.
- The passive from Lesson 21 also removes the agent from view, though it keeps
  the sense that somebody did the thing. An intransitive verb makes no such
  claim at all.

### ～てしまう
<!-- id: g2-l18-teshimau -->

**What it does.** Carries two meanings that share one form. The first is
completion, carrying out a plan with determination and bringing the action to
its culmination point. The second is a lack of premeditation or control, where
something regrettable happened or you did something you had no intention of
doing. Both readings turn on the gap between what was intended and how things
actually came out.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| any verb | te-form + しまう | 食べる → 食べてしまう |
| verb whose te-form voices to で | で + しまう | 読む → 読んでしまう |
| する verb | して + しまう | 注文する → 注文してしまう |

しまう itself conjugates as a u-verb, so 〜てしまいます, 〜てしまいました,
〜てしまって all behave as expected. Speech contracts the whole thing.

| full form | contraction |
|---|---|
| 落としてしまいました | 落としちゃいました |
| 落としてしまった | 落としちゃった |
| 頼んでしまいました | 頼んじゃいました |
| 頼んでしまった | 頼んじゃった |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 日記を全部読んでしまいました。 | Nikki wo zenbu yonde shimaimashita. | I read the diary all the way through. |
| 宿題を今すぐやってしまいます。 | Shukudai wo ima sugu yatte shimaimasu. | I am going to get the homework finished right away. |
| 新しいスカートを汚してしまいました。 | Atarashii sukaato wo yogoshite shimaimashita. | I went and got my new skirt dirty. |
| 留守番電話のメッセージを消してしまいました。 | Rusuban denwa no messeeji wo keshite shimaimashita. | I accidentally erased the message on the answering machine. |
| 友だちのラジカセを壊してしまいました。 | Tomodachi no rajikase wo kowashite shimaimashita. | I broke my friend's cassette player, unfortunately. |
| ポップコーンを全部食べちゃった。 | Poppukoon wo zenbu tabechatta. | I ate the whole lot of popcorn. |

**Notes**

- Which of the two readings applies is genuinely undecided by the grammar. The
  assumptions the speaker and listener bring settle it, and many sentences carry
  both at once. ケーキを食べてしまいました (Keeki wo tabete shimaimashita) is "I
  finished the cake" if you set out to eat it, and "I ended up eating the cake"
  if you knew you were supposed to leave it alone.
- しまう attaches to the affirmative te-form, so it can only report that
  something regrettable did happen. There is no way to say "unfortunately I did
  not do it" with しまう.
- The contractions ちゃう and じゃう are casual speech. They conjugate as u-verbs,
  giving 食べちゃいます, 食べちゃった, 飲んじゃって.
- The completion reading pairs naturally with 全部, 最後まで and 今すぐ, which push
  the sentence towards finishing rather than towards regret.

**Contrast**

- 〜ておく from Lesson 15 also handles an action carried to completion, with the
  point being preparation for what comes next. てしまう looks at the finishing
  itself.
- 〜ばよかったです names an alternative you did not take. てしまう reports the thing
  you actually did and adds the regret to it.

### ～と
<!-- id: g2-l18-to -->

**What it does.** Says that whenever the first situation holds, the second one
follows. The first clause usually gives the cause and the second gives the
effect, and the link between them runs on its own once the first situation is
in place. Most と sentences state a regular consequence, and some describe a
cause-effect link between two specific events.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | present tense short form + と | 春になる → 春になると |
| negative verb | ない form + と | 押さない → 押さないと |
| い-adjective | present tense short form + と | 高い → 高いと |
| な-adjective | stem + だ + と | 静かだ → 静かだと |
| noun | noun + だ + と | 学生だ → 学生だと |

The whole sentence is `clause A + と + clause B`, read as "whenever A happens, B
happens too". Genki's grammar-section examples are all verb clauses, which is
where the pattern does most of its work. Adjective and noun clauses before と
are fine too, and the lesson's own practice section uses one in 恥ずかしいと顔が
赤くなる.

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| このスイッチを押すと、エアコンがつきます。 | Kono suicchi wo osu to, eakon ga tsukimasu. | If you press this switch, the air conditioner comes on. |
| カーテンを開けると部屋が明るくなります。 | Kaaten wo akeru to heya ga akaruku narimasu. | When you open the curtains, the room gets bright. |
| 夕方になると外が暗くなります。 | Yuugata ni naru to soto ga kuraku narimasu. | When evening comes, it gets dark outside. |
| 冷たい牛乳を飲むと、おなかが痛くなります。 | Tsumetai gyuunyuu wo nomu to, onaka ga itaku narimasu. | Whenever I drink cold milk, I get a stomachache. |
| 船に乗ると気分が悪くなります。 | Fune ni noru to kibun ga waruku narimasu. | I get sick whenever I go on a boat. |
| 子供の時、冬になるとよく転びました。 | Kodomo no toki, fuyu ni naru to yoku korobimashita. | When I was a child, I used to fall over a lot whenever winter came. |
| 山田さんが手伝ってくれると助かります。 | Yamada-san ga tetsudatte kureru to tasukarimasu. | It will be a great help if Mr Yamada gives me a hand. |

**Notes**

- The clause before と is always in the present tense, no matter when the events
  took place. The second clause can be present or past, so a habit from your
  childhood comes out as present + と + past.
- The event in the second clause has to follow the event in the first. 牛乳を買う
  とスーパーに行きます (Gyuunyuu wo kau to suupaa ni ikimasu) is wrong, because going
  to the supermarket is not a consequence that comes after the buying.
- An adjective idea in the second clause is usually expressed as a change, so
  い-adjective base + くなる and な-adjective base + になる are very common in the
  second half. 冬になると部屋が寒くなります is the ordinary phrasing, and the plain
  adjective in 冬になると部屋が寒いです is much less usual. See Lesson 10 for
  adjective + なる.
- Most と sentences state a regularity, and と also links two specific events.
  山田さんが手伝ってくれると助かります is about one particular occasion, and it is a と
  sentence all the same.
- Because the consequence is automatic, と suits machines, seasons, physical
  reactions and rules of thumb.

**Contrast**

- 〜たら from Lesson 17 also links two events, and it reads as a one-off
  condition by default. と leans towards the regular consequence while still
  covering specific events. Beyond this lesson, と resists a request, an
  invitation or a command in the second clause, where たら takes all three
  freely.
- 〜ば comes in Lesson 22 and states a general condition without insisting on
  inevitability.

### ～ながら
<!-- id: g2-l18-nagara -->

**What it does.** Joins two verbs to say that both actions happen at the same
time. The verb carrying ながら is the background action, and the verb after it is
the main one that the sentence is really about.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| any verb | ます-stem + ながら | 聞きます → 聞きながら |
| ru-verb | drop る, add ながら | 食べる → 食べながら |
| する verb | stem + しながら | 注文します → 注文しながら |
| 来る | き + ながら | 来ます → 来ながら |

The second verb takes any form it likes, including past, negative, te-form and
the noun-modifying forms.

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| ラジカセで音楽を聞きながら日記を書きます。 | Rajikase de ongaku wo kikinagara nikki wo kakimasu. | I write my diary while listening to music on the cassette player. |
| ポップコーンを食べながら映画を見ました。 | Poppukoon wo tabenagara eiga wo mimashita. | I watched a movie while eating popcorn. |
| ジョンさんは働きながら大学に通っています。 | Jon-san wa hatarakinagara daigaku ni kayotte imasu. | John goes to university while holding down a job. |
| 家賃のことを考えながら歩いていました。 | Yachin no koto wo kangaenagara aruite imashita. | I was walking along thinking about the rent. |
| 歌を歌いながらシャンプーをします。 | Uta wo utainagara shanpuu wo shimasu. | I shampoo my hair while singing. |

**Notes**

- Both verbs must describe actions by the same person. ながら cannot say that you
  did one thing while somebody else did another.
- The main action goes in the second half. 音楽を聞きながら勉強する is studying with
  music on, and 勉強しながら音楽を聞く is listening to music with some studying going
  on in the background.
- Both actions have to be sustained over the same stretch of time. A momentary
  action such as 転ぶ makes a poor ながら verb.

**Contrast**

- 〜てから from Lesson 17 puts one action after the other. ながら overlaps them.
- 〜ている describes a single action in progress. ながら needs two.

### ～ばよかったです
<!-- id: g2-l18-ba-yokatta -->

**What it does.** Expresses regret about a course of action you did not take. It
translates as "I wish I had done it" or "I should have done it". The negative
version, なければよかったです, regrets something you did do.

**Formation**

Every verb takes the ば-form regularly, with no exceptions, and the form is built
from the present tense short forms.

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| affirmative ru-verb | drop the final -u, add -eba | 食べる (tabe-ru) → 食べれば (tabe-reba) |
| affirmative u-verb | drop the final -u, add -eba | 行く (ik-u) → 行けば (ik-eba), 待つ → 待てば, 買う → 買えば |
| する | drop the final -u, add -eba | する → すれば |
| くる | drop the final -u, add -eba | くる → くれば |
| negative | drop the final い of the ない-form, add ければ | 食べない → 食べなければ, 行かない → 行かなければ, しない → しなければ, こない → こなければ |

The regret pattern is then `ば-form + よかったです`, and the casual version drops
です.

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| もっと早く謝ればよかったです。 | Motto hayaku ayamareba yokatta desu. | I should have apologised sooner. |
| 冷蔵庫に牛乳を入れておけばよかったです。 | Reizouko ni gyuunyuu wo irete okeba yokatta desu. | I should have put the milk in the refrigerator. |
| あの時、山田さんに頼めばよかったです。 | Ano toki, Yamada-san ni tanomeba yokatta desu. | I should have asked Mr Yamada for the favour back then. |
| 日記を外に置かなければよかったです。 | Nikki wo soto ni okanakereba yokatta desu. | I wish I had not left my diary outside. |
| あんなにポップコーンを食べなければよかった。 | Anna ni poppukoon wo tabenakereba yokatta. | I wish I had not eaten that much popcorn. |
| 家賃を先に払えばよかったです。 | Yachin wo saki ni haraeba yokatta desu. | I should have paid the rent first. |

**Notes**

- The ば-form appears here only inside this fixed regret pattern. Its use as a
  general conditional waits until Lesson 22.
- The regret is real and often quite heavy. It goes with 恥ずかしい, どうしよう and
  apologies, and it is not a light "oh well".
- よかった is the past of いい, so the whole thing is literally "it would have been
  good if". That explains why the ば clause names the thing that did not happen.
- The negative side is the one learners forget. To regret an action you took,
  negate the verb, since 食べなければよかった is the regret about eating.

**Contrast**

- ～てしまう reports the event that actually took place and colours it with
  regret. ばよかったです points at the alternative you failed to take. 食べてしまい
  ました and 食べなければよかったです describe the same evening from two angles.
- 〜たらよかった and 〜ばよかった are close in meaning, and Genki teaches the ば
  version here.

## Practice prompts

1. (g2-l18-transitivity-pairs) Give 10 short scenes involving a door, a light, a
   window, a computer and a kettle, and ask for both the transitive and the
   intransitive description of each.
2. (g2-l18-transitivity-pairs) Present 8 sentences, half of them wrong because
   an intransitive verb has been given を, and ask which are wrong and why.
3. (g2-l18-transitivity-pairs) Produce 6 pairs of ている sentences from the same
   verb pair, and state for each whether it describes an action in progress or a
   resulting state.
4. (g2-l18-teshimau) Write 8 sentences about mishaps at a part-time job, then
   restate each in the casual ちゃう or じゃう contraction.
5. (g2-l18-teshimau) Give 5 sentences that are ambiguous between completion and
   regret, and for each supply the context that forces one reading.
6. (g2-l18-to) Produce 8 cause-effect sentences using machines, seasons and
   physical reactions, with the second clause phrased as a change using くなる or
   になる where an adjective is involved.
7. (g2-l18-to) Present 6 と sentences, two of which break the rule that the
   second event must follow the first, and ask which are wrong.
8. (g2-l18-nagara) Write 8 sentences describing two simultaneous actions by the
   same person, then supply 2 wrong sentences where the actors differ.
9. (g2-l18-ba-yokatta) Give 10 verbs and ask for both the affirmative and the
   negative ば-form.
10. (g2-l18-ba-yokatta) Write 6 regrets about a day that went badly, mixing
    affirmative and negative ば-forms.
11. (g2-l18-teshimau, g2-l18-ba-yokatta) Take 5 accidents described with てしまう
    and rewrite each as the matching ばよかったです regret.
12. (g2-l18-transitivity-pairs, g2-l18-to, g2-l18-nagara) Write a short account
    of an evening shift at a restaurant that uses all three patterns.
