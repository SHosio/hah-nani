# Genki II Lesson 20 (Mary's Shopping)

## Overview

Lesson 19 raised the other person with honorifics. This chapter does the
opposite move and lowers the speaker, in two separate ways that learners
routinely merge into one. Extra-modest expressions swap ordinary verbs for a
fixed set of stylized ones (おります, 参ります, 申します, いたします, ございます) and
signal deference to whoever is listening, which is why they saturate shop and
announcement Japanese. Humble expressions use the お + stem + する frame and
lower one specific action of yours because that action lands on somebody you
respect. The remaining four points are unrelated to respect language and to
each other, covering "without doing", embedded questions, naming an unfamiliar
thing, and ease or difficulty of doing something.

## Grammar

### Extra-modest Expressions
<!-- id: g2-l20-extra-modest -->

**What it does.** Replaces a handful of everyday verbs with stylized modest
counterparts so that everything you say about yourself sounds understated. The
deference goes to whoever is listening, and it ignores anyone else who happens
to appear in the sentence. English does the same job by tacking "sir" or
"ma'am" onto an utterance. Job interviews, formal self-introductions, station
announcements and shop counters are where these live.

**Formation**

| ordinary verb | extra-modest verb | polite form |
|---|---|---|
| いる | おる | おります |
| 行く／来る | 参る | 参ります |
| 言う | 申す | 申します |
| する | 致す | いたします |
| 食べる／飲む | 頂く | いただきます |
| ある | ござる | ございます |
| 〜ている | 〜ておる | 〜ております |
| 〜です | 〜でござる | 〜でございます |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 私はスミスと申します。 | Watakushi wa Sumisu to moushimasu. | My name is Smith, sir/ma'am. |
| 係の者がすぐ参ります。 | Kakari no mono ga sugu mairimasu. | The person in charge will be with you shortly. |
| 支店は三階でございます。 | Shiten wa san-gai de gozaimasu. | The branch office is on the third floor. |
| 黒いスニーカーはございません。 | Kuroi suniikaa wa gozaimasen. | We do not carry the sneakers in black. |
| 大学で日本語を勉強しております。 | Daigaku de nihongo wo benkyou shite orimasu. | I have been studying Japanese at university. |
| それでは、こちらで交換いたします。 | Soredewa, kochira de koukan itashimasu. | In that case, we will exchange it here for you. |

**Notes**

- These verbs are almost always used in the long ます form. The whole point is
  politeness towards the listener, so a plain 参る or 申す in casual speech is
  odd outside set phrases.
- They pair with the stiffer first-person word 私 (watakushi) rather than the
  everyday わたし.
- They are for your own actions, and by extension for your family or the
  company you work for when you are speaking to an outsider. 私の父は来週参ります
  is fine when talking to a customer.
- Applying them to the listener or to an outsider is wrong, since the effect is
  to put the subject in a modest light. ×先生はあした学校に参りますか。
- ございます and でございます are the most stylized of the set and are rare outside
  business situations. かしこまりました and 少々お待ちください belong to the same
  register and show up in the same conversations.
- 頂く appears in this list for 食べる and 飲む, and again in the humble list for
  もらう. The two uses are separate entries in the lesson's word list.

**Contrast**

- Honorifics from Lesson 19 (いらっしゃる, お帰りになる, お〜になる) raise the subject,
  who is someone else. These lower you. Applying an honorific to yourself is
  the classic beginner mistake, and applying an extra-modest verb to your
  professor is the mirror image of it.
- Humble expressions below also lower you, but they do it because your action
  touches a particular person you respect. Extra-modest verbs answer only to
  whoever is listening.

### Humble Expressions
<!-- id: g2-l20-humble -->

**What it does.** Describes an action of yours that is performed out of respect
for somebody, by dressing the verb in the frame お + verb stem + する. The
sentence lowers you and raises the person your action lands on, who is usually
underlined by context as a professor, a boss or a customer.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| ordinary verb | お + ます-stem + する | 貸す → お貸しする |
| ordinary verb | お + ます-stem + する | 送る → お送りする |
| する-compound verb | prefix ご or お, no stem pattern | 案内する → ご案内する, 電話する → お電話する |
| もらう | special verb | いただく |
| あげる | special verb | さしあげる |
| visiting or asking | special verb | 伺う |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 部長に係の者の名前をお伝えしました。 | Buchou ni kakari no mono no namae wo otsutae shimashita. | I (humbly) passed the name of the person in charge on to the manager. |
| お客様を空港までお送りします。 | Okyakusama wo kuukou made ookuri shimasu. | I will (humbly) see the guest off to the airport. |
| あした先生の研究室に伺います。 | Ashita sensei no kenkyuushitsu ni ukagaimasu. | I will (humbly) visit my professor's office tomorrow. |
| 先生に扇子を差し上げました。 | Sensei ni sensu wo sashiagemashita. | I (humbly) gave my professor a folding fan. |
| 先生に新しい小説を貸していただきました。 | Sensei ni atarashii shousetsu wo kashite itadakimashita. | My professor kindly lent me a new novel. |
| 店の場所はこちらでご説明します。 | Mise no basho wa kochira de gosetsumei shimasu. | I will (humbly) explain where the shop is over here. |
| お待たせしました。 | Omatase shimashita. | Sorry to have kept you waiting. |

**Notes**

- Not every verb takes this frame. Use the ones you have actually heard rather
  than coining お + stem + する for anything.
- する-compounds break the pattern. They keep their own shape and simply take
  the prefix ご or お, as in ご紹介する, ご案内する, ご説明する, お電話する.
- 伺う does double duty for visiting somebody and for asking somebody something.
- The book explicitly discourages te-form + さしあげる in the sense of "doing
  something for somebody", because announcing that you are doing a favour comes
  across as condescending. お + stem + する is the acceptable version, so
  地図をお見せしました rather than 地図を見せてさしあげました.
- The pattern also covers members of your own group. 父はお客さんにお茶をお出ししました
  works because the action goes from inside your circle to an outsider.
- Occasionally a humble form means "instead of you" or "saving you trouble",
  as in テレビをおつけしましょうか offered to a guest.

**Contrast**

- Extra-modest expressions are a fixed vocabulary list and show deference to
  the listener. Humble expressions are a productive pattern and show deference
  to the person affected by your action, who might not be in the room. 私は
  メアリーと申します is modest towards whoever hears it, while 私は先生に本を
  お返ししました raises the professor.
- The three-way summary the book gives at the end of the section is worth
  memorising as a unit. Honorifics raise the subject, extra-modest expressions
  lower you towards your listener, and humble expressions lower you while
  raising the person your action reaches.
- いただく sits in both categories. In 先生に本をいただきました it is the humble
  もらう, and in いただきます before a meal it is the extra-modest 食べる.

### ～ないで
<!-- id: g2-l20-naide -->

**What it does.** Marks an action that was skipped while another one went
ahead. The clause names what you did not do, and the main clause names what you
did instead.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| any verb | short negative ない + で | 寝ない → 寝ないで |
| u-verb | 〜わない → 〜わないで | 使わない → 使わないで |
| する | しない → しないで | 電話しない → 電話しないで |
| 来る | こない → こないで | 来ない → 来ないで |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| ゆうべは寝ないで、レポートを書きました。 | Yuube wa nenaide, repooto wo kakimashita. | Last night I wrote a report without getting any sleep. |
| 地図を見ないで、空港まで歩きました。 | Chizu wo minaide, kuukou made arukimashita. | I walked to the airport without looking at a map. |
| 朝ご飯を食べないで、学校に来ました。 | Asagohan wo tabenaide, gakkou ni kimashita. | I came to school without eating breakfast. |
| 店に電話しないで、返品しに行きました。 | Mise ni denwa shinaide, henpin shi ni ikimashita. | I went to return the item without calling the shop first. |
| 傘を持たないで出かけて、困りました。 | Kasa wo motanaide dekakete, komarimashita. | I went out without taking an umbrella and got into trouble. |

**Notes**

- The present tense ない is used for past actions too. なかったで does not exist,
  and the tense is carried entirely by the main clause verb.
- The same 〜ないで shape is the negative request from Genki I, as in 心配しないで
  ください, so context decides which reading applies.
- ～ずに is the written and stiffer equivalent, with せずに as the irregular form
  for する. It is worth recognising even though this lesson does not teach it.

**Contrast**

- 〜なくて states a cause. 日本語が分からなくて困りました is about why you were in
  trouble, while 辞書を使わないで読みました is about the circumstances of reading.
- 〜ないで pairs one clause against another. A simple negative sentence such as
  寝ませんでした just reports that the action did not happen.

### Questions within Larger Sentences
<!-- id: g2-l20-embedded-questions -->

**What it does.** Packs a question inside a bigger sentence so you can say
things like "I do not know when the test is" or "I do not remember whether she
came". The embedded question keeps its own question word and gets a か at the
end, and the outer sentence supplies a verb such as 知っています, 分かりません or
覚えていません.

**Formation**

| embedded clause | ending | example |
|---|---|---|
| contains a question word (だれ, なに, どこ, いつ, どうして) | short form + か | どこに住んでいるか |
| yes/no question, no question word | short form + かどうか | 旅行に行くかどうか |
| な-adjective or noun predicate, present tense | drop だ, then か／かどうか | 一番上手か, 学生かどうか |
| explanatory 〜んですか | 〜の + か | どうして来なかったのか |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 係の者が何時に戻るか分かりません。 | Kakari no mono ga nanji ni modoru ka wakarimasen. | I do not know what time the person in charge is coming back. |
| この字を何と読むか教えてください。 | Kono ji wo nan to yomu ka oshiete kudasai. | Please tell me how this character is read. |
| ゆうべどこで晩ご飯を食べたか覚えていません。 | Yuube doko de bangohan wo tabeta ka oboete imasen. | I do not remember where I had dinner last night. |
| この店で返品できるかどうか聞いてみます。 | Kono mise de henpin dekiru ka dou ka kiite mimasu. | I will ask whether this shop takes returns. |
| あの人が宇宙人かどうか分かりません。 | Ano hito ga uchuujin ka dou ka wakarimasen. | I have no idea whether that person is a space alien. |
| どちらのハイヒールが安いか覚えていますか。 | Dochira no haihiiru ga yasui ka oboete imasu ka. | Do you remember which pair of high heels was cheaper? |

**Notes**

- The embedded clause is in the short form. A ですか never goes inside, so
  どこに住んでいますか becomes どこに住んでいるか.
- だ drops before か and かどうか when the clause ends in a な-adjective or a noun
  in the present tense. 上手だか and 学生だかどうか are both wrong. The past だった
  stays.
- The subject inside the embedded clause tends to take が where the direct
  question would have used は. 私はたけしさんが何を食べたか知っています.
- Explanatory ん です goes in as の + か, so どうして来なかったんですか becomes
  どうして来なかったのか分かりません.
- In speech many people use plain か for yes/no questions too, dropping the
  どうか.

### name という item
<!-- id: g2-l20-to-iu -->

**What it does.** Introduces a person or thing by a name you assume the
listener does not recognise, wrapping the name in という and following it with a
category noun that tells the listener what kind of thing it is.

**Formation**

| pattern | shape | example |
|---|---|---|
| naming a thing | (name) という (item) | 「月の音」という小説 |
| naming a person | (name) という (person noun) | 田中という者 |
| asking for the name | 何という (item) | 何という店 |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 「月の音」という小説を読みました。 | "Tsuki no oto" to iu shousetsu wo yomimashita. | I read a novel called "The Sound of the Moon". |
| ハナという猫を飼っています。 | Hana to iu neko wo katte imasu. | I have a cat called Hana. |
| 田中という者から電話がありました。 | Tanaka to iu mono kara denwa ga arimashita. | There was a call from someone called Tanaka. |
| これは扇子という物です。 | Kore wa sensu to iu mono desu. | This is a thing called a sensu. |
| 何という空港に着きますか。 | Nan to iu kuukou ni tsukimasu ka. | What is the airport we arrive at called? |

**Notes**

- The point of the pattern is the listener's unfamiliarity. If you both know
  the shop, just say the shop's name.
- The item noun is a plain category word such as 店, 歌, 犬, 人, 者, 小説 or 町.
  It carries the grammatical role, so particles attach to it rather than to the
  name.
- という is 言う in the plain form, but it does not shift for tense here.
  「花」という歌を知っていましたか keeps という unchanged.
- Written Japanese often puts the name in 「」, while speech relies on という
  alone to mark where the name ends.
- 〜というのは introduces a definition, as in 扇子というのは日本のかざる物です, which is
  the same という doing a slightly different job.

### ～やすい／～にくい
<!-- id: g2-l20-yasui-nikui -->

**What it does.** Turns a verb into an adjective meaning easy to do or hard to
do. The result behaves as an ordinary い-adjective, so it takes past, negative
and noun-modifying forms like any other.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| u-verb | ます-stem + やすい／にくい | 使う → 使いやすい, 読む → 読みにくい |
| ru-verb | ます-stem + やすい／にくい | 食べる → 食べにくい |
| する | し + やすい／にくい | 生活する → 生活しやすい |
| 来る | き + やすい／にくい | 来る → 来にくい |
| inflection | conjugates as an い-adjective | 使いやすかった, 読みにくくない |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| このスニーカーは歩きやすいです。 | Kono suniikaa wa arukiyasui desu. | These sneakers are easy to walk in. |
| ハイヒールは歩きにくいです。 | Haihiiru wa arukinikui desu. | High heels are hard to walk in. |
| この小説は字が小さくて読みにくかったです。 | Kono shousetsu wa ji ga chiisakute yominikukatta desu. | The print in this novel was small, so it was hard to read. |
| この町はとても生活しやすいです。 | Kono machi wa totemo seikatsu shiyasui desu. | This town is very easy to live in. |
| このかばんは重くて持ちにくいです。 | Kono kaban wa omokute mochinikui desu. | This bag is heavy and awkward to carry. |
| 先生の説明は分かりやすかったです。 | Sensei no setsumei wa wakariyasukatta desu. | The professor's explanation was easy to follow. |

**Notes**

- The subject does not have to be the thing being acted on. It can be a place
  you find easy to do something in or a tool you find easy to do something
  with, as in このグラスは飲みにくいです.
- With action verbs these suffixes report psychological ease or reluctance.
  They sit badly where the difficulty is physical or a matter of success rates.
- The book's own comparison makes the split visible. 漢字は覚えにくい is about
  kanji anxiety, while 漢字を覚えるのは難しい is about the sheer complexity of the
  task. この雑誌は買いにくい means you feel awkward buying it, while
  この雑誌を買うのは難しい means copies are hard to come by.
- やすい standing alone as a word means cheap. The ordinary adjective for easy
  is やさしい, so 安い本 and 読みやすい本 are unrelated claims.

**Contrast**

- 〜のは難しい／易しい describes the objective difficulty of a task. 〜にくい／〜やすい
  describes how the doer experiences it.
- 〜すぎる (Lesson 12) reports excess rather than difficulty. 重すぎる says the bag
  is too heavy, while 持ちにくい says carrying it is a nuisance.

## Practice prompts

1. (g2-l20-extra-modest) Give 8 ordinary polite sentences about the speaker and
   ask for the extra-modest rewrite, covering all of おります, 参ります, 申します,
   いたします, いただきます, ございます, 〜ております and 〜でございます.
2. (g2-l20-extra-modest) Write a short shop-counter exchange in which a clerk
   uses at least four extra-modest verbs, plus かしこまりました and 少々お待ちください.
3. (g2-l20-extra-modest) Present 6 sentences, two of which wrongly apply an
   extra-modest verb to the listener or to an outsider, and ask which are wrong
   and why.
4. (g2-l20-humble) Provide 8 dictionary-form verbs and ask for the お + stem +
   する humble form, including two する-compounds that take ご or お instead.
5. (g2-l20-humble) Produce 6 sentences where the speaker does something for a
   professor or a customer, using お送りする, お伝えする, お貸しする, 伺う, いただく and
   差し上げる once each.
6. (g2-l20-extra-modest, g2-l20-humble) Give 10 sentences and ask the learner to
   label each one honorific, extra-modest or humble, and to say whose profile is
   raised and whose is lowered.
7. (g2-l20-naide) Write 8 sentences pairing a skipped action with what was done
   instead, half in the past tense, and confirm that ない never becomes なかった.
8. (g2-l20-naide) Present 6 pairs of 〜ないで and 〜なくて sentences and ask which
   member of each pair states a cause.
9. (g2-l20-embedded-questions) Turn 10 direct questions into embedded ones,
   five with question words taking か and five yes/no questions taking かどうか,
   including two clauses ending in a noun or な-adjective.
10. (g2-l20-embedded-questions) Write 6 sentences using 知っています,
    覚えていません, 分かりません and 決めましょう as the outer verb, marking the
    embedded subject with が.
11. (g2-l20-to-iu) Build 8 sentences introducing an unfamiliar shop, song, pet,
    novel or person by name, and add two 何という questions.
12. (g2-l20-yasui-nikui) Give 10 verbs and ask for both the やすい and the にくい
    forms, then use four of them in past-tense sentences.
13. (g2-l20-yasui-nikui) Present 6 situations and ask whether 〜にくい or
    〜のは難しい fits, explaining the psychological versus objective split.
14. (g2-l20-humble, g2-l20-embedded-questions, g2-l20-to-iu) Write a short
    monologue by a shop assistant helping a customer return a pair of shoes,
    using all three patterns.
