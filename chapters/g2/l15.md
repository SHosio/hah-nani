# Genki II Lesson 15 (A Trip to Nagano)

## Overview

The chapter is built around planning a trip, and the first three points are the
grammar of planning. The volitional form is the casual counterpart of ましょう
for suggesting something to a friend. Wrapping that form in と思っています turns a
suggestion into a stated intention. ～ておく covers the errands you run before
the trip so that everything is ready when it starts. The fourth point is
unrelated and much larger, since it lets a whole sentence sit in front of a noun
and describe it, which is how Japanese does relative clauses.

| point | id | one-line summary |
|---|---|---|
| Volitional Form | g2-l15-volitional | Casual "let's" and "shall I". |
| Volitional Form + と思っています | g2-l15-volitional-to-omotte-imasu | I have decided to do it. |

## Grammar

### Volitional Form
<!-- id: g2-l15-volitional -->

**What it does.** The plain, casual equivalent of ましょう. You use it to propose
a plan to a close friend, or to offer to do something yourself. It is the form
you reach for among people you already speak casually with.

**Formation**

| verb class | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| ru-verb | drop る, add よう | 食べる → 食べよう, 見る → 見よう |
| u-verb | final -u sound → -o sound + う | 行く → 行こう, 話す → 話そう, 待つ → 待とう, 読む → 読もう |
| u-verb ending in う | う → お + う | 買う → 買おう, 誘う → 誘おう |
| u-verb, remaining endings | same -o sound rule | 泳ぐ → 泳ごう, 遊ぶ → 遊ぼう, 死ぬ → 死のう, 取る → 取ろう |
| する | replace with しよう | 予約する → 予約しよう |
| 来る | replace with こよう | 来る → こよう |

Two uses, distinguished by the question particle:

| pattern | function | example |
|---|---|---|
| volitional | a suggestion or a declaration of what you will do | 行こう |
| volitional + か | asking the listener's opinion of your offer or suggestion | 行こうか |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 週末、映画館に行こう。 | Shuumatsu, eigakan ni ikou. | Let's go to the movie theater this weekend. |
| 山田さんも誘おうか。 | Yamada-san mo sasaou ka. | Shall we invite Yamada too? |
| 旅館を予約しようか。 | Ryokan wo yoyaku shiyou ka. | Shall I reserve an inn? |
| 一緒に地図を見よう。 | Issho ni chizu wo miyou. | Let's look at the map together. |
| あしたは早く起きよう。 | Ashita wa hayaku okiyou. | Let's get up early tomorrow. |
| 昼ごはんはそばにしよう。 | Hirugohan wa soba ni shiyou. | Let's make it soba for lunch. |

**Notes**

- The vowel change is the same one used for the plain negative stem, moved to
  the -o row instead of the -a row, so 行か-ない and 行こ-う share the same slot.
- 来る is こよう, and the kanji reading shifts from き to こ, which is the usual
  place to slip.
- Because it is casual, it does not belong in speech to a teacher, a customer or
  anyone you would use ます forms with. Use ましょう there instead.
- Adding よ makes the suggestion pushier and more enthusiastic, as in 行こうよ.
- The か question here is a genuine request for the other person's opinion, so
  it softens an offer rather than demanding an answer.

**Contrast**

- ましょう is the polite form of the same function, and ましょうか matches
  volitional + か.
- ～ませんか (Genki I) is a softer invitation that leaves the listener more room
  to decline than a bare volitional does.

### Volitional Form + と思っています
<!-- id: g2-l15-volitional-to-omotte-imasu -->

**What it does.** States a determination. The volitional carries the intention
and と思っています reports that you are holding it, so the sentence means you have
already made up your mind to do the thing.

**Formation**

| input | rule | reading |
|---|---|---|
| volitional + と思っています | a decision already made and still standing | 観光しようと思っています |
| volitional + と思います | a decision made on the spot, at the moment of speaking | 観光しようと思います |
| plain present + と思います | a prediction or an opinion, not an intention | 観光すると思います |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 夏休みに長野を観光しようと思っています。 | Natsuyasumi ni Nagano wo kankou shiyou to omotte imasu. | I am planning to go sightseeing in Nagano over the summer break. |
| 週末に新しいジャケットを探そうと思っています。 | Shuumatsu ni atarashii jaketto wo sagasou to omotte imasu. | I am planning to look for a new jacket this weekend. |
| 古い家具を捨てようと思っています。 | Furui kagu wo suteyou to omotte imasu. | I have decided to throw out the old furniture. |
| じゃあ、銀行でお金を下ろそうと思います。 | Jaa, ginkou de okane wo orosou to omoimasu. | In that case I think I will withdraw some money at the bank. |
| 姉は来年卒業すると思います。 | Ane wa rainen sotsugyou suru to omoimasu. | I think my older sister will graduate next year. |
| 旅館の割引券を使おうと思っています。 | Ryokan no waribikiken wo tsukaou to omotte imasu. | I am planning to use the inn discount coupon. |

**Notes**

- The verb inside stays in the plain volitional. と思っています never takes a
  ます form in front of it.
- The difference between 思います and 思っています is how long the decision has been
  yours. 思います fits a decision you are announcing as you make it, 思っています
  fits one you arrived at earlier.
- A volitional and a plain present in front of と思います are not
  interchangeable. 働こうと思います is what you intend to do, 働くと思います is what you
  guess will happen.
- Since 思っています describes a state of mind rather than a momentary thought, it
  is also the natural form for reporting somebody else's plans.
- The expression notes on page 82 add plain present + 予定です for something
  already on the calendar, which is a schedule rather than a determination.

**Contrast**

- ～たいです expresses a wish. Volitional + と思っています expresses a decision you
  have already taken, which is a stronger claim.
- ～つもりです (Genki I Lesson 10) is close in meaning and slightly more formal in
  feel, and it attaches to the plain present rather than to the volitional.

### ～ておく
<!-- id: g2-l15-teoku -->

**What it does.** Marks an action done ahead of time, in preparation for
something you know is coming. The point is not the action alone, it is that you
do it now so that things are ready later.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| any verb | te-form + おく | 調べる → 調べておく |
| polite | te-form + おきます | 予約する → 予約しておきます |
| past | te-form + おきました | 買う → 買っておきました |
| spoken contraction | ておく → とく | 予約しておく → 予約しとく |
| spoken contraction, voiced | でおく → どく | 読んでおく → 読んどく |

おく itself conjugates as a u-verb, so おいて, おいた and おかない all behave
normally.

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 締め切りの前にレポートを書いておきます。 | Shimekiri no mae ni repooto wo kaite okimasu. | I will write the report before the deadline. |
| 旅館はもう予約しておきました。 | Ryokan wa mou yoyaku shite okimashita. | I already reserved the inn in advance. |
| 電池を買っておいたほうがいいですよ。 | Denchi wo katte oita hou ga ii desu yo. | You had better buy batteries beforehand. |
| あしたの発表の練習をしておきます。 | Ashita no happyou no renshuu wo shite okimasu. | I will practise for tomorrow's presentation ahead of time. |
| 行く前に旅館の場所を調べておかなきゃいけません。 | Iku mae ni ryokan no basho wo shirabete okanakya ikemasen. | I have to look up where the inn is before we go. |
| 地図、かばんに入れとくね。 | Chizu, kaban ni iretoku ne. | I will put the map in the bag for you. |

**Notes**

- There is almost always an implied future event the preparation is aimed at,
  and it often appears in a から or ので clause in the same sentence.
- The contraction is spoken, not written. Keep ておきます in a report or an email.
- It stacks with the obligation patterns, giving 〜ておかなきゃいけません and
  〜ておかなければいけません, both of which are common when listing errands.
- Beyond this lesson, ておく also covers leaving something in its current state,
  as in そのままにしておく. The preparation sense is the one Genki teaches here.

**Contrast**

- ～てある (Lesson 21) describes the resulting state somebody has left behind,
  where ておく describes the act of preparing. 予約しておきました is what I did,
  予約してあります is how things now stand.
- ～てしまう (Lesson 18) also attaches to the te-form but marks completion or
  regret, so it points backwards rather than forwards.

### Using Sentences to Qualify Nouns
<!-- id: g2-l15-noun-qualification -->

**What it does.** Puts a whole sentence in front of a noun to say what kind of
thing it is, exactly the way an adjective does in おもしろい本. Japanese has no
relative pronoun, so nothing stands where English would put "that", "which" or
"who". The qualifying clause simply sits immediately before the noun, and the
two together behave as one big noun phrase.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb, present affirmative | short form + noun | 庭が見える部屋 |
| verb, present negative | short negative + noun | 日本で売っていない電池 |
| verb, past affirmative | た-form + noun | きのう予約した旅館 |
| verb, past negative | なかった form + noun | 誰も来なかったパーティー |
| subject inside the clause | が, never は | 姉がかいた絵 |
| nothing between clause and noun | no の, no pause | 姉がかいた絵 (not 姉がかいたの絵) |

The clause is a full sentence in short form, so the tense inside it is chosen
independently of the tense that ends the whole sentence.

| whole phrase used as | example |
|---|---|
| subject | きのう予約した旅館は駅の近くです。 |
| object | 姉がかいた絵を見ました。 |
| after a particle | 庭が見える部屋に泊まりたいです。 |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| きのう予約した旅館は駅の近くです。 | Kinou yoyaku shita ryokan wa eki no chikaku desu. | The inn I reserved yesterday is near the station. |
| 姉がかいた絵が一番好きです。 | Ane ga kaita e ga ichiban suki desu. | I like the painting my older sister drew best of all. |
| 庭が見える広い部屋に泊まりたいです。 | Niwa ga mieru hiroi heya ni tomaritai desu. | I want to stay in a spacious room the garden is visible from. |
| 友だちが教えてくれた番組を見ました。 | Tomodachi ga oshiete kureta bangumi wo mimashita. | I watched the program a friend told me about. |
| 日本で売っていない電池を探しています。 | Nihon de utte inai denchi wo sagashite imasu. | I am looking for a battery that is not sold in Japan. |
| 去年卒業した学生から手紙が来ました。 | Kyonen sotsugyou shita gakusei kara tegami ga kimashita. | A letter came from a student who graduated last year. |
| 外国人が一番よく行く町はどこですか。 | Gaikokujin ga ichiban yoku iku machi wa doko desu ka. | Which town do foreigners go to most? |

**Notes**

- The verb of the qualifying clause is always in a short form, even when the
  sentence as a whole ends in です or ます. 予約しました旅館 is wrong.
- When the person performing the action appears inside the clause, they take が
  and not は. は marks the topic of a whole sentence, and a qualifying clause is
  not a whole sentence, it is a chunk inside one. 私は買った本 and 私が買った本 mean
  very different things.
- The qualified noun can play any role inside the clause. In きのう買った本 it is
  the object of 買う, in 庭が見える部屋 it is where the seeing happens, and the
  clause looks the same either way.
- Word order is the mirror image of English. The description comes first and the
  noun it describes comes last, so build the clause before you commit to the
  noun.
- Once assembled, the phrase is just a noun and takes は, が, を, に or any other
  particle as normal, and can appear anywhere a plain noun could.
- Nothing intervenes between the clause and the noun. Learners who have got used
  to 日本の本 tend to insert の here, and it is never correct.
- Outside this lesson you will also see の standing in for が inside the clause
  (私の作った料理). Genki teaches が here, so use が.

**Contrast**

- ～から and ～ので clauses also come before something, but they attach to a whole
  clause and give a reason. A qualifying clause attaches to a single noun and
  describes it.
- ～と言っていました (Lesson 8) embeds a quoted sentence, which stays a sentence.
  A qualifying clause has been demoted to the role of a modifier.
- い-adjectives and noun + の qualify nouns in the same slot, so 広い部屋, 旅館の部屋
  and 庭が見える部屋 are three versions of one structure.

   weekend trip, half plain volitional and half volitional + か, and note which
   ones would need ましょう if the listener were a teacher.
3. (g2-l15-volitional-to-omotte-imasu) Write 6 sentences stating plans for the
   next year using volitional + と思っています, drawing on this chapter's travel and
   school vocabulary.
4. (g2-l15-volitional-to-omotte-imasu) Present 6 pairs contrasting
   volitional + と思います with plain present + と思います on the same verb, and label
   which is an intention and which is a prediction.
5. (g2-l15-teoku) List 8 preparations for a trip to Nagano using ～ておきます, each
   with a から or ので clause naming what the preparation is for.
6. (g2-l15-teoku) Rewrite 6 ～ておく sentences into their spoken ～とく form,
   including at least two verbs whose te-form is voiced.
7. (g2-l15-noun-qualification) Give 10 short sentences and a noun for each, and
   ask for the two combined into a qualifier + noun phrase.
8. (g2-l15-noun-qualification) Produce 8 sentences where the qualifying clause
   has its own subject, and mark why が appears there instead of は.
9. (g2-l15-noun-qualification) Write 6 qualifier phrases that mix tense and
   polarity inside the clause (past affirmative, present negative, past
   negative) while the main sentence stays in the polite present.
10. (g2-l15-volitional, g2-l15-volitional-to-omotte-imasu, g2-l15-teoku,
    g2-l15-noun-qualification) Write a short casual conversation between two
    friends planning a trip that uses all four patterns at least once.
