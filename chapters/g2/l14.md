# Genki II Lesson 14 (Valentine's Day)

## Overview

The chapter is built around gift-giving, so its centre of gravity is the
あげる／くれる／もらう trio, which forces you to track who is closer to you before you
can pick a verb at all. ほしい comes first and runs into the same social wall from
a different side, since wanting something is a private sensation you are not
allowed to assert on someone else's behalf. かもしれません lets you hedge a guess,
たらどうですか lets you suggest a course of action, and the も／しか pair lets you
comment on a quantity as generous or as disappointing, which is exactly what
counting presents calls for.

## Grammar

### ほしい
<!-- id: g2-l14-hoshii -->

**What it does.** Says that you want a thing. It behaves as an い-adjective, so
the thing wanted is marked with が and the whole word conjugates like 高い. It
covers objects, where 〜たい from Lesson 11 covers actions.

**Formation**

| pattern | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| affirmative | (私は) X が ほしいです | 皿がほしいです |
| negative | X が ほしくないです | ネクタイがほしくないです |
| past | X が ほしかったです | ぬいぐるみがほしかったです |
| past negative | X が ほしくなかったです | 指輪がほしくなかったです |
| quoting someone else | X が ほしいと言っています | 漫画がほしいと言っています |
| guessing about someone else | X が ほしいでしょう | 兄は新しい皿がほしいでしょう |
| observing someone else | X を ほしがっている | ぬいぐるみをほしがっています |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 私は新しい指輪がほしいです。 | Watashi wa atarashii yubiwa ga hoshii desu. | I want a new ring. |
| 子供の時、大きいぬいぐるみがほしかったです。 | Kodomo no toki, ookii nuigurumi ga hoshikatta desu. | When I was a child I wanted a big stuffed animal. |
| 高いものはほしくないです。 | Takai mono wa hoshikunai desu. | I do not want expensive things. |
| メアリーさんはマフラーがほしいと言っていました。 | Mearii-san wa mafuraa ga hoshii to itte imashita. | Mary said she wanted a winter scarf. |
| 兄は新しいビデオカメラをほしがっています。 | Ani wa atarashii bideo kamera wo hoshigatte imasu. | My older brother wants a new camcorder. |

**Notes**

- が marks the thing wanted, in negatives as much as in affirmatives.
  ネクタイがほしくないです is the plain version. は is also available in a negative when
  you want the contrastive reading, as in 高いものはほしくないです, which sets expensive
  things off against everything else.
- ほしい is a private predicate. Japanese treats an inner sensation as knowable
  only by the person feeling it, so a bare ほしいです stating what somebody else
  wants is wrong, and that covers the listener as well as a third party.
  ×あなたはマフラーがほしいです and ×メアリーさんはマフラーがほしいです both need a device.
  Quote them with 〜と言っています, hedge with でしょう or かもしれません, or use ほしがる.
- Questions are the exception, because they ask the person to report their own
  sensation. 何がほしいですか and 子供の時、何がほしかったですか are the normal way to ask.
- ほしがる conjugates as an u-verb and normally appears as ほしがっている, reporting an
  observation the speaker currently believes. Its object takes を where ほしい
  takes が, which is the detail people miss.
- The same restriction applies to the other private predicates met so far,
  かなしい, うれしい and いたい, and to 〜たい, whose observation form is たがる.

**Contrast**

- 〜たい attaches to a verb stem and wants an action. ほしい is a standalone
  adjective and wants a thing. 車がほしいです versus 車を買いたいです.
- ほしい states what you want. A bare 〜がほしいです aimed at the person holding the
  thing lands about as bluntly as it would in English, so in a shop you ask for
  the object itself with 〜をください from Lesson 2. Asking someone to perform an
  action takes another pattern again, 〜てくれませんか in Lesson 16, which attaches to
  a te-form. Beyond this lesson, the softened 〜がほしいんですが is a standard way of
  turning the desire into an indirect request.

### ～かもしれません
<!-- id: g2-l14-kamoshiremasen -->

**What it does.** Marks a statement as a possibility. You use it when you do not
actually know how things stand and are prepared to venture a guess anyway. It is
weaker and less confident than でしょう from Lesson 12.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | short form + かもしれません | 降る → 降るかもしれません |
| verb, past | past short form + かもしれません | 降った → 降ったかもしれません |
| verb, negative | ない form + かもしれません | 来ない → 来ないかもしれません |
| い-adjective | short form + かもしれません | 高い → 高いかもしれません |
| な-adjective | drop だ, add かもしれません | きらいだ → きらいかもしれません |
| noun + です | drop だ／です, add かもしれません | 学生だ → 学生かもしれません |
| な-adjective or noun, past | keep だった, add かもしれません | けちだった → けちだったかもしれません |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 週末は雪が降るかもしれません。 | Shuumatsu wa yuki ga furu kamoshiremasen. | It might snow at the weekend. |
| 大家さんは今いないかもしれません。 | Ooyasan wa ima inai kamoshiremasen. | The landlord may not be in right now. |
| このネクタイはちょっと高いかもしれません。 | Kono nekutai wa chotto takai kamoshiremasen. | This necktie may be a little expensive. |
| 山田さんの奥さんは日本人じゃないかもしれません。 | Yamada-san no okusan wa nihonjin ja nai kamoshiremasen. | Mr Yamada's wife may not be Japanese. |
| 田中さんは去年プロポーズしたかもしれません。 | Tanaka-san wa kyonen puropoozu shita kamoshiremasen. | Mr Tanaka may have proposed last year. |
| あの人は日本語の先生だったかもしれません。 | Ano hito wa nihongo no sensei datta kamoshiremasen. | That person may have been a Japanese teacher. |

**Notes**

- It sits after short forms in every tense and polarity, so the tense is carried
  by the predicate in front of it and かもしれません itself never changes for tense.
- After a な-adjective or a noun in the present affirmative, だ disappears. 学生だ
  かもしれません is wrong, 学生かもしれません is right. This is the same drop でしょう
  triggers.
- The drop is bounded by that description, so it does not reach the past.
  けちだったかもしれません and 学生だったかもしれません keep their だった, and the negative keeps
  its じゃない. Learners who have just learned the rule tend to shave the だった as
  well.
- かもしれない is the short form and is what casual speech uses. Spoken Japanese
  shortens it further to かも.

**Contrast**

- でしょう claims the speaker thinks something is probable. かもしれません only
  claims it is possible, so it commits you to much less.
- 〜と思います reports your opinion. かもしれません reports the state of your
  uncertainty, which is why it works well for excuses and warnings.

### あげる／くれる／もらう
<!-- id: g2-l14-ageru-kureru-morau -->

**What it does.** Describes a gift moving between people. Japanese has two verbs
for giving, and the choice is decided by direction. Picture yourself at the
centre of a set of circles, with the person you are talking to next to you and
everyone else further out. A thing travelling away from the centre is あげる, a
thing travelling towards the centre is くれる. Because the listener sits inside
the circle with you, くれる covers a gift arriving at the listener as well as one
arriving at you. もらう describes the same event as くれる from the receiver's side.

**Formation**

| pattern | direction | example |
|---|---|---|
| (giver) は／が (recipient) に あげる | away from the centre | 私は妹にぬいぐるみをあげました |
| (giver) は／が (recipient) に くれる | towards the centre, so to me or to you | 母が私にマフラーをくれました |
| (recipient) は／が (giver) に／から もらう | towards the receiver | 私は兄から漫画をもらいました |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 私は妹にぬいぐるみをあげました。 | Watashi wa imouto ni nuigurumi wo agemashita. | I gave my little sister a stuffed animal. |
| バレンタインデーに彼にチョコレートをあげるつもりです。 | Barentaindee ni kare ni chokoreeto wo ageru tsumori desu. | I intend to give him chocolate on Valentine's Day. |
| 母が私にマフラーをくれました。 | Haha ga watashi ni mafuraa wo kuremashita. | My mother gave me a winter scarf. |
| 両親が新しい皿をくれるかもしれません。 | Ryoushin ga atarashii sara wo kureru kamoshiremasen. | My parents might give me new plates. |
| 田中さんはあなたに何をくれましたか。 | Tanaka-san wa anata ni nani wo kuremashita ka. | What did Mr Tanaka give you? |
| 私は兄から漫画をもらいました。 | Watashi wa ani kara manga wo moraimashita. | I received comic books from my older brother. |
| ホワイトデーにお返しをもらいました。 | Howaitodee ni okaeshi wo moraimashita. | I received a thank-you gift on White Day. |

**Notes**

- With あげる and くれる the giver is the subject and takes は or が, and the
  recipient takes に. With もらう the recipient is the subject and the giver takes
  に or から.
- 私に is routinely dropped with くれる, since the verb already points the gift
  inwards and the speaker is the default reading.
- くれる also handles a gift that reaches the listener, so 田中さんはあなたに何をくれましたか
  works even though nothing came to the speaker. Keep that に phrase in place,
  since dropping it makes the sentence read as a gift to the speaker.
- あげる cannot point at the speaker. ×友だちが私にチョコレートをあげました is wrong,
  because a thing arriving at the centre is くれる territory. The sentence you
  want is 友だちが私にチョコレートをくれました.
- When both people are outside your circle, あげる is the default. くれる is
  possible for a transaction between two other people only when you identify
  strongly with the receiver, typically immediate family or a close friend, and
  you feel you benefited too.
- The same closeness logic blocks もらう when you are the giver. ×あなたは私から
  チョコレートをもらいましたか is wrong, because the perspective would have to sit with
  the recipient while you are standing at the other end.
- もらう for a third-party transaction is fine once you take the recipient's
  perspective, as in 妹はおばさんにぬいぐるみをもらいました.

**Contrast**

- あげる versus くれる is one event described from opposite ends of the same
  arrow. 私は友だちにチョコレートをあげました and 友だちが私にチョコレートをくれました are
  different events, and swapping the verbs without swapping the people is the
  classic error.
- くれる versus もらう is one event with the subject moved. 両親が私に皿をくれました and
  私は両親に皿をもらいました report the same thing, and the particles switch to match.
  くれる foregrounds the giver's kindness, もらう foregrounds what you ended up with.
- Remember that くれる cannot take the speaker as its giver. ×私が母にマフラーをくれました
  is not a sentence, because the giver has to stand further out than the
  recipient and you are already at the centre. 私は母にマフラーをあげました is the
  sentence you want.

### ～たらどうですか
<!-- id: g2-l14-tara-dou-desu-ka -->

**What it does.** Offers advice or a recommendation, close to English "why don't
you" or "how about". The た at the front is the same ending as the past tense
short form in the affirmative.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| any verb | past short form (た-form) + らどうですか | 送る → 送ったらどうですか |
| する verb | したらどうですか | 相談する → 相談したらどうですか |
| 来る | きたらどうですか | 来る → きたらどうですか |
| casual | shorten to たらどう or たら | 休んだらどう？／休んだら？ |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 大家さんに相談したらどうですか。 | Ooyasan ni soudan shitara dou desu ka. | Why don't you consult the landlord? |
| 履歴書を電子メールで送ったらどうですか。 | Rirekisho wo denshi meeru de okuttara dou desu ka. | How about sending your resume by e-mail? |
| もう少し休んだらどうですか。 | Mou sukoshi yasundara dou desu ka. | Why don't you rest a little longer? |
| そのクラブはあきらめたらどうですか。 | Sono kurabu wa akirametara dou desu ka. | How about giving up on that club? |
| 奥さんに指輪を買ったらどうですか。 | Okusan ni yubiwa wo kattara dou desu ka. | Why don't you buy your wife a ring? |

**Notes**

- The tone can come out critical, implying the listener should have done the
  thing already. The safe rule is to keep it for situations where someone has
  actually asked you for advice.
- It cannot extend an invitation. To get a friend to join you the pattern is
  一緒にコンサートに行きませんか, and ×一緒にコンサートに行ったらどうですか is wrong, because it
  advises the friend to go while you stay out of the picture.
- The た is a real conjugation, so verbs whose past tense ends in だ keep the だ.
  遊んだらどうですか, 読んだらどうですか.

**Contrast**

- 〜ませんか invites, and the speaker joins in. たらどうですか advises, and the
  speaker stays out of it.
- 〜ほうがいいです from Lesson 12 states which option is better and is blunter about
  it. たらどうですか floats the idea as a question.

### number + も / number + しか + negative
<!-- id: g2-l14-mo-shika -->

**What it does.** Comments on a quantity instead of just stating it. も after a
number word says the amount is surprisingly large, and しか with a negative
predicate says it is disappointingly small.

**Formation**

The lesson builds on the ordinary counting structure, where the number word
follows the noun and its particle.

| pattern | rule | reading |
|---|---|---|
| plain count | noun + が／を + number | チョコレートを五個買いました |
| number + も | noun + が／を + number + も, affirmative predicate | as many as |
| number + しか | noun + が／を + number + しか, negative predicate | only that few |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 妹はチョコレートを十個ももらいました。 | Imouto wa chokoreeto wo jukko mo moraimashita. | My little sister got as many as ten chocolates. |
| 去年、漫画を三十冊も買いました。 | Kyonen, manga wo sanjussatsu mo kaimashita. | Last year I bought thirty comic books, count them, thirty. |
| うちには犬が三匹しかいません。 | Uchi ni wa inu ga sanbiki shika imasen. | We have only three dogs at home. |
| ビデオカメラは一台しかありません。 | Bideo kamera wa ichidai shika arimasen. | There is only one camcorder. |
| ネクタイを三本ももらいました。 | Nekutai wo sanbon mo moraimashita. | I received three neckties, as many as three. |
| 今日は一時間しかありません。 | Kyou wa ichijikan shika arimasen. | Today I have only one hour. |

**Notes**

- しか is ungrammatical with an affirmative predicate. The negative is part of
  the pattern, and the sentence still translates as a positive statement in
  English.
- Both particles attach to the number word, so が and を stay where they were.
  漫画を一冊しか持っていません keeps を on 漫画 and puts しか on 一冊.
- Both are evaluations rather than facts about the number. 三匹も and 三匹しか
  describe the same three animals, and the difference is entirely in what the
  speaker thinks of that.
- Watch the counter sound changes when you build these, since 個, 冊, 台, 匹 and
  本 all shift after certain numbers.

**Contrast**

- だけ also means "only" and takes an affirmative predicate, so 三匹だけいます is a
  neutral report where 三匹しかいません is a complaint.
- The も here is the quantity も, unrelated to the "also" も and to the
  question-word も of だれも and なにも.

## Practice prompts

1. (g2-l14-hoshii) Produce 8 sentences about wanting gifts from this chapter's
   noun list, mixing affirmative, negative and past forms, and mark which
   particle each takes.
2. (g2-l14-hoshii) Give 8 sentences about what somebody else wants, covering the
   listener as well as a third person, spread across 〜と言っています, でしょう and
   ほしがっている, and point out the が to を switch.
3. (g2-l14-kamoshiremasen) Supply 10 predicates across all four word classes and
   ask for the かもしれません form, including two な-adjectives and two nouns in the
   present so the だ-drop gets tested and two more in the past so the surviving
   だった gets tested.
4. (g2-l14-kamoshiremasen) Write 6 hedged guesses about a party, then rewrite
   each with でしょう and describe how the confidence changes.
5. (g2-l14-ageru-kureru-morau) Describe 8 gift transactions, each once with the
   giver as subject and once with the receiver as subject, keeping the particles
   correct in both versions.
6. (g2-l14-ageru-kureru-morau) Present 6 sentences, three of them wrong (くれる
   with the speaker as giver, もらう with the speaker as giver, あげる with the
   speaker as recipient), and ask which fail and why. Include one correct くれる
   sentence whose recipient is the listener.
7. (g2-l14-tara-dou-desu-ka) Write 8 pieces of advice for a friend with a
   housing, work or relationship problem, using this chapter's verbs.
8. (g2-l14-tara-dou-desu-ka) Give 5 situations and ask whether たらどうですか or
   ませんか is the right choice, including at least two invitations.
9. (g2-l14-mo-shika) Build 10 quantity sentences using 個, 冊, 台, 匹 and 本, five
   with も and five with しか plus a negative, and check the counter readings.
10. (g2-l14-hoshii, g2-l14-ageru-kureru-morau, g2-l14-mo-shika) Write a short
    Valentine's Day monologue where the speaker says what they wanted, what they
    gave and received, and how many chocolates turned up.
