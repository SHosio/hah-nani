# Genki II Lesson 23 (Good-bye)

## Overview

The final chapter of Genki II closes the arc that Lessons 21 and 22 opened. The
passive put you on the receiving end of an action, the causative put you in
charge of one, and the causative-passive combines them into being forced to act
by somebody else. That form is the hardest in the book, mostly because it stacks
two conjugations and because u-verbs take a shortened variant. The other four
points are unrelated to it and to each other, covering concession, decisions, a
time limit, and a way of turning verbs into nouns.

## Grammar

### Causative-passive Sentences
<!-- id: g2-l23-causative-passive -->

**What it does.** Says you were made to do something by somebody else, and that
you were not willing. It is built by taking the causative form and then making
it passive, so it carries both the instigator of the causative and the put-upon
subject of the passive.

**Formation**

| verb class | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| ru-verb | drop -ru, add -saserareru | 食べる → 食べさせられる |
| u-verb | drop -u, add -asareru, with う going to わ | 待つ → 待たされる, 飲む → 飲まされる, 書く → 書かされる, 買う → 買わされる |
| u-verb ending in す | drop -u, add -aserareru | 話す → 話させられる |
| する | replace with させられる | 我慢する → 我慢させられる |
| 来る | replace with こさせられる | 来る → こさせられる |

Sentence pattern: 「(person forced) は or が, (person forcing) に, then the
action and the causative-passive verb」. The action keeps whatever particle the
plain verb would take, so it is 車を洗わされる with を and 質問に答えさせられる
with に.

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 私は母に野菜を食べさせられました。 | Watashi wa haha ni yasai wo tabesaseraremashita. | My mother made me eat vegetables. |
| 先輩に一時間待たされました。 | Senpai ni ichijikan matasaremashita. | My senior kept me waiting for an hour. |
| 部長にお酒を飲まされました。 | Buchou ni osake wo nomasaremashita. | My manager made me drink. |
| 子供の時、母にピアノを習わされました。 | Kodomo no toki, haha ni piano wo narawasaremashita. | As a child I was made to learn the piano. |
| 面接で難しい質問に答えさせられました。 | Mensetsu de muzukashii shitsumon ni kotaesaseraremashita. | I was made to answer difficult questions at the interview. |

**Notes**

- Reluctance is built into the meaning. There is no neutral causative-passive,
  so it always reports something you would rather not have done.
- The short u-verb form 飲まされる is what people actually say. The long form
  飲ませられる is grammatical but stilted.
- Verbs ending in す never take the short form. 話す gives 話させられる and nothing
  else. Beyond this lesson, the reason is that the short -asare- suffix would
  stack a second さ onto the stem.
- The person who forced you takes に, exactly as the agent does in the passive.

**Contrast**

- Three forms of the same verb sit close together and get confused. 食べさせる is
  "I made someone eat", 食べさせられる is "I was made to eat", and 食べられる is both
  "it got eaten on me" and "can eat".
- The tell is who the subject is. Causative takes the instigator as subject,
  causative-passive takes the person forced.

### ～ても
<!-- id: g2-l23-temo -->

**What it does.** 「A ても B」 says that B is still true in the case of A, which
English renders as "B, even if A". It concedes the first clause and asserts the
second anyway.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | te-form + も | 買う → 買っても |
| い-adjective | drop い, add くても | 悲しい → 悲しくても (× 悲しかっても) |
| な-adjective | stem + でも | 元気(な) → 元気でも (× 元気だっても) |
| noun | noun + でも | 学生 → 学生でも (× 学生だっても) |
| verb, negative | ない → なくても | 買わない → 買わなくても |
| い-adjective, negative | くない → くなくても | 悲しくない → 悲しくなくても |
| な-adjective, negative | じゃない → じゃなくても | 元気じゃない → 元気じゃなくても |
| noun, negative | じゃない → じゃなくても | 学生じゃない → 学生じゃなくても |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 雨が降っても、犬を散歩に連れていきます。 | Ame ga futte mo, inu wo sanpo ni tsurete ikimasu. | I take the dog for a walk even if it rains. |
| 高くても、この辞書を買います。 | Takakute mo, kono jisho wo kaimasu. | I will buy this dictionary even if it is expensive. |
| 元気でも、無理をしないでください。 | Genki de mo, muri wo shinai de kudasai. | Please do not overdo it, even if you feel fine. |
| 学生でも、この仕事ができます。 | Gakusei de mo, kono shigoto ga dekimasu. | Even a student can do this job. |
| 悪口を言われても、我慢します。 | Waruguchi wo iwarete mo, gaman shimasu. | Even if people talk behind my back, I will put up with it. |
| いくら説明しても、分かってくれません。 | Ikura setsumei shite mo, wakatte kuremasen. | No matter how much I explain, they do not understand. |
| アルバイトが忙しくても、毎日日本語を練習しました。 | Arubaito ga isogashikute mo, mainichi nihongo wo renshuu shimashita. | I practised Japanese every day even when my part-time job was busy. |

**Notes**

- The ても clause carries no tense of its own. It looks the same whether the main
  clause is present or past, so 忙しくても covers "even if I am busy" and "even
  when I was busy" alike.
- いくら〜ても and どんなに〜ても mean "no matter how much", and are the natural way
  to intensify the concession.
- The main clause is unrestricted, so commands, requests and invitations are all
  fine after ても.
- With a question word, ても becomes "whatever" or "whenever". 何を食べても is
  "whatever I eat".
- 悲しかっても, 元気だっても and 学生だっても are the forms to avoid. Adjectives and
  nouns take no past form in front of も.

**Contrast**

- 〜のに (Lesson 22) also concedes, but only about something that actually
  happened, and it carries the speaker's annoyance. ても is neutral and works
  for hypotheticals.
- 〜たら and 〜ば say the second clause follows *because* of the first. ても says
  it happens *despite* the first. 高くても買います is "I will buy it even if it is
  expensive", while 高かったら買いません is "I will not buy it if it is expensive".
- Verb たら and ても forms look alike (買ったら / 買っても), so they are easy to
  confuse. Adjective forms are easy to tell apart (悲しかったら / 悲しくても).

### ～ことにする
<!-- id: g2-l23-koto-ni-suru -->

**What it does.** Reports a decision the speaker made, in the sense of "decide
to do . . ." It follows the short form present tense of a verb, and that verb
can be negated.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb, affirmative | short form present + ことにする | 留学する → 留学することにする |
| verb, negative | short form negative + ことにする | 吸わない → 吸わないことにする |
| regular practice | short form present or negative + ことにしている | 読む → 読むことにしている |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 留学することにしました。 | Ryuugaku suru koto ni shimashita. | I have decided to study abroad. |
| たばこを吸わないことにしました。 | Tabako wo suwanai koto ni shimashita. | I have decided not to smoke. |
| 面接を受けることにしました。 | Mensetsu wo ukeru koto ni shimashita. | I decided to take the interview. |
| 今年は海外旅行をしないことにします。 | Kotoshi wa kaigai ryokou wo shinai koto ni shimasu. | I am deciding not to travel abroad this year. |
| 電車の中では本を読むことにしています。 | Densha no naka de wa hon wo yomu koto ni shite imasu. | I make it a rule to read a book on the train. |
| エレベーターは使わないことにしています。 | Erebeetaa wa tsukawanai koto ni shite imasu. | I have made it my rule not to use the elevator. |
| 今度の連休は温泉に行くことにしよう。 | Kondo no renkyuu wa onsen ni iku koto ni shiyou. | Let us go ahead and visit a hot spring over the coming holiday. |

**Notes**

- The verb before ことに is always the short form present, never past, no matter
  when the decision was made.
- Announcing a decision with ことにしました sounds settled and slightly formal,
  which is why it fits news like quitting a job or moving away. The plain
  present ことにします works too, for a decision you are announcing as you take it.
- **ことにしている** is the same construction turned into a standing practice.
  You made up your mind once and have stuck to that determination. The nuance is
  deliberate self-discipline, so it is wrong for habits that merely happen; use
  ～ています for those.
- **ことにしよう** is the volitional, used in place of a plain 行こう when the
  suggestion follows some deliberation, so it carries a sense of having weighed
  the idea first.

**Contrast**

- 〜ことになる is the counterpart where the decision was not yours. 転勤すること
  になりました means it was decided for you, typically by your employer. Japanese
  speakers often prefer it even for their own choices, because it sounds less
  self-assertive.
- 〜つもりです states an intention rather than a settled decision.
- 〜ています alone reports what you do. ことにしています reports what you have
  decided to do. 走っています is "I run", 走ることにしています is "I make myself run".

### ～まで
<!-- id: g2-l23-made -->

**What it does.** 「A まで B」 means "B till A". A describes the change that
coincides with or causes the end of B, so it marks the point at which the main
action stops.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | short form present, affirmative + まで | やむ → やむまで |
| noun | noun + まで | 三時 → 三時まで |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 雨がやむまで、待ちましょう。 | Ame ga yamu made, machimashou. | Let us wait until the rain stops. |
| 卒業するまで、アルバイトを続けます。 | Sotsugyou suru made, arubaito wo tsuzukemasu. | I will keep my part-time job until I graduate. |
| 日本に来るまで、寿司を食べたことがありませんでした。 | Nihon ni kuru made, sushi wo tabeta koto ga arimasen deshita. | Until I came to Japan I had never eaten sushi. |
| 友だちが戻ってくるまで、ここにいます。 | Tomodachi ga modotte kuru made, koko ni imasu. | I will stay here until my friend comes back. |
| バスが来るまで、みんなでおしゃべりをしていました。 | Basu ga kuru made, minna de oshaberi wo shite imashita. | We chatted together until the bus came. |

**Notes**

- The verb in the まで clause is always present tense and always affirmative,
  even when the whole sentence describes the past. Putting it in the past is the
  most common mistake with this pattern.
- That verb is usually one of the "change" kind introduced in Lesson 7, since it
  names the change that brings the main action to an end.
- When the subject of the まで clause differs from the subject of the main
  clause, it takes が rather than は. 父が帰ってくるまで、私は待っていました shows
  the switch, and バスが来るまで above does the same.
- Beyond this lesson, notice that the main clause normally describes something
  that lasts, since まで gives the point where it runs out.

**Contrast**

- までに means "by", a deadline for a single action, and takes the opposite kind
  of main clause. 三時までに終わります is "I will finish by three", while
  三時まで働きます is "I will work until three".

### ～方
<!-- id: g2-l23-kata -->

**What it does.** A noun-forming suffix meaning "the way in which the action is
performed" or "how to do it". It attaches to a verb stem and the result behaves
as an ordinary noun.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| u-verb | ます-stem + 方 | 泳ぐ → 泳ぎ方 |
| ru-verb | ます-stem + 方 | 考える → 考え方 |
| verb with a particle-marked noun | noun + の + verb-stem + 方 | お弁当を作る → お弁当の作り方 |
| compound する verb | noun + の + し方 | 部屋を掃除する → 部屋の掃除のし方 |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 田中さんの話し方はとてもやさしいです。 | Tanaka-san no hanashikata wa totemo yasashii desu. | The way Tanaka speaks is very gentle. |
| お弁当の作り方を母に習いました。 | Obentou no tsukurikata wo haha ni naraimashita. | I learned from my mother how to make a boxed lunch. |
| タイヤの換え方が分かりません。 | Taiya no kaekata ga wakarimasen. | I do not know how to change a tire. |
| 犬の世話のし方を教えてもらいました。 | Inu no sewa no shikata wo oshiete moraimashita. | Somebody taught me how to look after a dog. |
| すみません、駅への行き方を教えていただけませんか。 | Sumimasen, eki e no ikikata wo oshiete itadakemasen ka. | Excuse me, could you tell me how to get to the station? |

**Notes**

- A noun that would normally take を or another particle with the verb switches
  to の before ～方. It is お弁当を作る but お弁当の作り方.
- The goal of movement normally marked with に can take への instead, so both
  駅の行き方 and 駅への行き方 are fine.
- Because ～方 is a noun, it takes は, を and the rest exactly as any noun does.
- With a compound する verb the し is kept, giving 掃除のし方 rather than 掃除し方.

**Contrast**

- ～方 is about manner or method. 〜ことができる (Lesson 13's potential) is about
  ability. 泳ぎ方を知っています is "I know how to swim" in the sense of knowing the
  technique, while 泳げます says you can actually do it.
