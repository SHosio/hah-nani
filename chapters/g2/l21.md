# Genki II Lesson 21 (Burglar)

## Overview

The chapter is organised around things that happen *to* you rather than things
you do. Passive sentences give you the grammar for being on the receiving end
of someone else's action, usually to your cost, which is why the chapter's
vocabulary is full of burglars, mosquitoes and complaints. ～てある covers the
opposite situation, walking into a state that somebody deliberately left
behind, and ～間に supplies the window of time during which either happened.
～てほしい turns the same outward focus into a request about other people's
behaviour. Adjective + する is the outlier, a plain transitive change-of-state
pattern that pairs with ～くなる／～になる from Lesson 10.

## Grammar

### Passive Sentences
<!-- id: g2-l21-passive -->

**What it does.** Recasts a sentence so the affected party becomes the subject.
In Genki the passive is introduced primarily as the *adversative* passive, where
the speaker is inconvenienced or harmed by what someone else did. The agent is
marked with に, and the resulting verb conjugates like a ru-verb regardless of
what the original verb was.

**Formation**

| verb class | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| ru-verb | drop る, add られる | 食べる → 食べられる |
| u-verb | final -u sound → -a sound + れる | 読む → 読まれる, 書く → 書かれる |
| u-verb ending in う | う → わ + れる | 言う → 言われる, 使う → 使われる |
| する | replace with される | 連絡する → 連絡される |
| 来る | replace with こられる | 来る → こられる |

Sentence pattern: 「(affected person) は (agent) に (thing) を (passive verb)」.
The affected person is the subject even though they did nothing.

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 私は母に日記を読まれました。 | Watashi wa haha ni nikki wo yomaremashita. | My mother read my diary (and I am not happy about it). |
| どろぼうにお金を盗まれました。 | Dorobou ni okane wo nusumaremashita. | I had my money stolen by a burglar. |
| 電車で隣の人に足を踏まれました。 | Densha de tonari no hito ni ashi wo fumaremashita. | The person next to me stepped on my foot on the train. |
| ゆうべ蚊に刺されました。 | Yuube ka ni sasaremashita. | I was bitten by a mosquito last night. |
| 弟にケーキを食べられました。 | Otouto ni keeki wo taberaremashita. | My little brother ate my cake on me. |
| 友だちに文句を言われました。 | Tomodachi ni monku wo iwaremashita. | My friend complained at me. |

**Notes**

- The agent takes に. Genki does not introduce によって at this stage.
- When the action is done to something you possess or to a body part, that
  thing keeps を and you stay the subject. 私は足を踏まれた, never 私の足は踏まれた.
- Every passive verb inflects as a ru-verb, so the negative is 読まれない, the
  past is 読まれた, and the te-form is 読まれて.
- う-verbs ending in う insert わ rather than あ. 言う becomes 言われる, not 言あれる.
- The English translation often needs "had my X done to me" rather than a plain
  English passive, because the nuisance is part of the meaning.

**Contrast**

- Potential forms of ru-verbs are identical to their passive forms. 食べられる
  is both "can eat" and "be eaten"; only context and the particles separate them.
- A neutral English passive ("this temple was built in 1650") is usually better
  served in Japanese by an active sentence or by ～てある, not by this pattern.

### ～てある
<!-- id: g2-l21-tearu -->

**What it does.** Describes a state that exists because someone deliberately
put it there, with the focus on the result rather than on who did it. The
implication is nearly always preparation, something arranged ahead of time for
a reason.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| transitive verb | te-form + ある | 貼る → 貼ってある |
| transitive verb | te-form + あります (polite) | 作る → 作ってあります |

The affected object is marked が or は, not を, because it has become the
subject of the state being described.

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| かべにポスターが貼ってあります。 | Kabe ni posutaa ga hatte arimasu. | A poster has been put up on the wall. |
| 晩ご飯が作ってあります。 | Bangohan ga tsukutte arimasu. | Dinner has been made and is ready. |
| ビールが冷蔵庫に入れてあります。 | Biiru ga reizouko ni irete arimasu. | Beer has been put in the fridge. |
| プレゼントはもう包んであります。 | Purezento wa mou tsutsunde arimasu. | The present has already been wrapped. |
| 窓が閉めてあります。 | Mado ga shimete arimasu. | The window has been closed (somebody closed it). |

**Notes**

- Transitive verbs only. There is no ～てある for 閉まる, 開く or 始まる.
- The を of the original sentence becomes が. 窓を閉める becomes 窓が閉めてある.
- Because deliberateness is built in, ～てある carries a quiet "so that we are
  ready" flavour even when nothing about purpose is said out loud.

**Contrast**

- 窓が閉まっています uses the intransitive verb with ～ている and simply reports
  that the window is shut, with no claim that anyone shut it on purpose.
  窓が閉めてあります says somebody shut it, deliberately, and it is still shut.
- ～ておく (Lesson 15) is the *action* of getting something ready in advance.
  ～てある is the *state* that action leaves behind. 冷やしておきました is what
  you did; 冷やしてあります is what the fridge now contains.

### ～間に
<!-- id: g2-l21-aidani -->

**What it does.** Marks the period during which something else happened. The
event in the main clause occupies a point inside the span described by the
～間に clause.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | ている form + 間に | 寝ている間に |
| noun | noun + の + 間に | 留守の間に |
| い-adjective | plain form + 間に | 若い間に |
| な-adjective | stem + な + 間に | 元気な間に |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 私が寝ている間に、どろぼうが入りました。 | Watashi ga nete iru aida ni, dorobou ga hairimashita. | A burglar got in while I was asleep. |
| お風呂に入っている間に、電話がありました。 | Ofuro ni haitte iru aida ni, denwa ga arimashita. | There was a phone call while I was in the bath. |
| 留守の間に、荷物が届きました。 | Rusu no aida ni, nimotsu ga todokimashita. | A package arrived while I was out. |
| 学生の間に、たくさん旅行したいです。 | Gakusei no aida ni, takusan ryokou shitai desu. | I want to travel a lot while I am a student. |

**Notes**

- The subject of the ～間に clause takes が, never は. 私が寝ている間に, not
  私は寝ている間に.
- Verbs almost always appear in the ている form here, because the clause has to
  describe an ongoing span rather than a single moment.
- The main clause event is typically brief and often outside the speaker's
  control, which is why this pattern sits so naturally in a chapter about
  burglars.

**Contrast**

- 間 without に means the main clause lasts the whole span, not a moment inside
  it. 授業の間、ずっと寝ていました (Jugyou no aida, zutto nete imashita) means you
  slept through the entire class. Swap in 間に and the sentence stops making
  sense, because sleeping is not a point event.
- ～時 (Lesson 16) locates an event at a time without any sense of a span being
  interrupted.

### Adjective + する
<!-- id: g2-l21-adj-suru -->

**What it does.** Turns an adjective into a transitive verb phrase meaning
"make something more X". The thing being changed takes を, and the speaker is
the one causing the change.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | い → く + する | 短い → 短くする |
| い-adjective (irregular) | いい → よく + する | いい → よくする |
| な-adjective | drop な, add に + する | きれいな → きれいにする |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 部屋をきれいにしました。 | Heya wo kirei ni shimashita. | I tidied the room up. |
| 音を小さくしてください。 | Oto wo chiisaku shite kudasai. | Please turn the sound down. |
| 髪を短くしました。 | Kami wo mijikaku shimashita. | I had my hair cut short. |
| 説明をもっと簡単にしてください。 | Setsumei wo motto kantan ni shite kudasai. | Please make the explanation simpler. |

**Notes**

- いい is irregular throughout its conjugation, so "improve" is よくする.
- The pattern also appears with 〜くしておく and 〜にしておく when the change is
  preparatory.
- With a noun instead of an adjective, 〜にする means "decide on" (コーヒーに
  します), which is a different pattern that happens to look similar.

**Contrast**

- ～くなる and ～になる (Lesson 10) are the intransitive counterparts. 部屋が
  きれいになった says the room became clean, with no agent. 部屋をきれいにした
  says you did it. The particle on the affected thing is the tell, が for なる
  and を for する.

### ～てほしい
<!-- id: g2-l21-tehoshii -->

**What it does.** States that you want somebody else to perform an action. The
person you want to act is marked with に, and ほしい itself behaves as an
い-adjective.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| affirmative | (person) に + te-form + ほしい | 手伝ってほしい |
| negative | (person) に + ない form + でほしい | 言わないでほしい |
| past | ほしい → ほしかった | 手伝ってほしかった |
| negative want | ほしい → ほしくない | 手伝ってほしくない |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 友だちに手伝ってほしいです。 | Tomodachi ni tetsudatte hoshii desu. | I want my friend to help me. |
| 政府に環境を守ってほしいです。 | Seifu ni kankyou wo mamotte hoshii desu. | I want the government to protect the environment. |
| 彼にもっと連絡してほしいです。 | Kare ni motto renraku shite hoshii desu. | I want him to get in touch more often. |
| だれにも言わないでほしいです。 | Dare ni mo iwanaide hoshii desu. | I would rather you did not tell anyone. |

**Notes**

- ほしい inflects as an い-adjective, so all four forms above are regular.
- Aimed at a superior this is blunt. 〜ていただきたい is the polite alternative,
  and Lesson 22 supplies 〜ていただけませんか for the same job.
- The negative attaches to the verb, not to ほしい. It is 言わないでほしい, and
  言ってほしくない means something subtly different, closer to "it is not the case
  that I want you to say it".

**Contrast**

- ～たい (Lesson 11) is wanting to do something yourself. 手伝いたい is "I want
  to help", 手伝ってほしい is "I want you to help".
- ほしい directly after a noun (Lesson 14) means wanting to possess a thing.
  車がほしい is "I want a car", and takes が rather than に.
- ～てもらいたい is close in meaning and slightly softer in tone.

## Practice prompts

1. (g2-l21-passive) Produce 8 sentences in which the speaker is inconvenienced
   by someone else's action, drawing the verbs from this chapter's u-verb and
   ru-verb lists. Half should involve a possession or body part keeping を.
2. (g2-l21-passive) Give 10 dictionary-form verbs from Lessons 13 to 21 and ask
   for the passive form, mixing ru-verbs, u-verbs ending in う, and the two
   irregulars.
3. (g2-l21-tearu) Describe a room that has been prepared for a guest using six
   ～てある sentences. Then rewrite each with the intransitive ～ている form and
   explain what changes.
4. (g2-l21-tearu) Present pairs such as 窓が閉まっている and 窓が閉めてある and ask
   which fits a given situation.
5. (g2-l21-aidani) Build 6 sentences where something happens during a stated
   span, using one verb, one noun, and one adjective attachment at minimum.
6. (g2-l21-aidani) Contrast 間 and 間に in 4 minimal pairs and state which is
   correct for each context.
7. (g2-l21-adj-suru) Give 8 situations needing a change of state and ask for
   both the する version and the なる version.
8. (g2-l21-tehoshii) Write 6 requests about other people's behaviour, two of
   them negative, and mark which would be rude directed at a professor.
9. (g2-l21-tehoshii) Contrast ～たい and ～てほしい across 5 situations where the
   English "want" is ambiguous.
10. (g2-l21-passive, g2-l21-aidani, g2-l21-tearu) Write a short burglary report
    in which the speaker returns home, using at least one of each pattern.
