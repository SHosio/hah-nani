# Genki II Lesson 13 (Looking for a Part-time Job)

## Overview

The first chapter of Genki II is built around a job hunt, and the grammar
serves that setting. Potential verbs are the heavyweight point, since telling an
employer what you can and cannot do is the whole conversation. ～し stacks up
several reasons at once, which is how you argue for or against taking a job.
～そうです and ～てみる both deal with incomplete information, one guessing what
something is like from the outside and the other committing to find out. なら
narrows a claim down to one item, and 一週間に三回 is the frame for saying how
often something happens.

| point | id | one-line summary |
|---|---|---|
| Potential Verbs | g2-l13-potential | Say that someone can do something. |
| ～し | g2-l13-shi | Line up two or more reasons. |

## Grammar

### Potential Verbs
<!-- id: g2-l13-potential -->

**What it does.** States that someone has the ability to do something, or that
something is possible. Japanese builds this into the verb itself rather than
adding a separate word for "can". The resulting verb always conjugates as a
ru-verb, whatever class it started in.

**Formation**

| verb class | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| ru-verb | drop る, add られる | 見る → 見られる, 食べる → 食べられる |
| u-verb | final -u sound → -e sound + る | 行く → 行ける, 読む → 読める, 買う → 買える |
| u-verb (more) | same rule across all endings | 泳ぐ → 泳げる, 待つ → 待てる, 遊ぶ → 遊べる, 取る → 取れる |
| する | replace with できる | 仕事をする → 仕事ができる |
| 来る | replace with こられる | 来る → こられる |

Once formed, it inflects like any ru-verb.

| form | example |
|---|---|
| present affirmative | 見られる／見られます |
| present negative | 見られない／見られません |
| past affirmative | 見られた／見られました |
| past negative | 見られなかった／見られませんでした |
| te-form | 見られて |

Particles shift when a verb goes potential.

| original | potential | note |
|---|---|---|
| 漢字を読む | 漢字が読める or 漢字を読める | を may stay or turn into が |
| 仕事をする | 仕事ができる | 仕事をできる is considered substandard |
| 山に登る | 山に登れる | particles other than を do not change |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 私はバイオリンが弾けます。 | Watashi wa baiorin ga hikemasu. | I can play the violin. |
| 弟は空手ができます。 | Otouto wa karate ga dekimasu. | My younger brother can do karate. |
| 着物は一人で着られません。 | Kimono wa hitori de kiraremasen. | I cannot put on a kimono by myself. |
| このカレーは辛すぎて、全部食べられませんでした。 | Kono karee wa karasugite, zenbu taberaremasen deshita. | This curry was too spicy and I could not finish it. |
| 広告の言葉が難しくて、読めませんでした。 | Koukoku no kotoba ga muzukashikute, yomemasen deshita. | The wording of the advertisement was difficult and I could not read it. |
| 雨だったので、山に登れませんでした。 | Ame datta node, yama ni noboremasen deshita. | It was raining, so we could not climb the mountain. |

**Notes**

- Potential forms of ru-verbs are long. Shorter ra-less forms exist for ru-verbs
  and for 来る, made with -れる instead of -られる, so 出る → 出れる, 見る → 見れる,
  来る → これる. They are spreading among younger speakers and are still treated
  as substandard, so keep them out of writing.
- できる is the potential of する and behaves specially. It takes が nearly all
  the time, which is why 仕事ができる is the normal shape.
- Verb dictionary form + ことができる carries the same meaning and belongs mostly
  to the written language. ギターを弾くことができます sits alongside ギターが弾けます.
  The negative is dictionary form + ことができない.
- For musical instruments in general, できる and やる do the work. 何か楽器が
  できますか asks whether you play anything at all, without naming the specific
  verb the instrument would take (ギターを弾く, サックスを吹く, ドラムをたたく).

**Contrast**

- ことができる is the long written equivalent of the potential verb. Same
  meaning, heavier register, no particle puzzle since the object keeps を.

### ～し
<!-- id: g2-l13-shi -->

**What it does.** Chains reasons together. から gives one reason for a
situation, and し lets you line up two or more of them before you state what
follows. It also leaves the door open, since a し clause implies that the reason
given is one of several.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | short form + し | 行くし, 来たし, 読まないし |
| い-adjective | short form + し | おもしろいし, 安かったし |
| な-adjective | stem + だ + し | 好きだし, 幸せだし |
| noun + です | noun + だ + し | 学生だし, 弁護士だし |

The whole pattern:

| pattern | shape |
|---|---|
| two or more reasons | (reason1) し、(reason2) し、(situation)。 |
| one reason, others implied | (reason) し、(situation)。 |
| situation first | (situation)。(reason) し、(reason) し。 |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 文法はおもしろいし、先生は厳しくないし、この授業が大好きです。 | Bunpou wa omoshiroi shi, sensei wa kibishikunai shi, kono jugyou ga daisuki desu. | The grammar is interesting and the teacher is not strict, so I love this class. |
| あの店はケーキがおいしいし、紅茶も安いし、よく行きます。 | Ano mise wa keeki ga oishii shi, koucha mo yasui shi, yoku ikimasu. | The cake at that shop is good and the tea is cheap too, so I go there often. |
| 彼は約束を守るし、まじめだし、みんなに人気があります。 | Kare wa yakusoku wo mamoru shi, majime da shi, minna ni ninki ga arimasu. | He keeps his promises and he is serious, so everyone likes him. |
| 物価が高いし、この町の生活は大変です。 | Bukka ga takai shi, kono machi no seikatsu wa taihen desu. | Prices are high, for one thing, so life in this town is hard. |
| きのうは悲しい一日でした。友だちが泣いていたし、雨も降っていたし。 | Kinou wa kanashii ichinichi deshita. Tomodachi ga naite ita shi, ame mo futte ita shi. | Yesterday was a sad day. A friend was in tears, and it rained as well. |
| 今日はレポートを書かなきゃいけないし、アルバイトもあるし、行けません。 | Kyou wa repooto wo kakanakya ikenai shi, arubaito mo aru shi, ikemasen. | I have to write a paper today and I have work too, so I cannot come. |

**Notes**

- し attaches to short forms, so だ appears with な-adjectives and with nouns but
  never with い-adjectives. おもしろいだし is wrong.
- A single し clause is not a mistake. It signals that you are giving one reason
  out of several and letting the listener fill in the rest.
- The situation can come first and the し clauses can trail behind it as
  afterthoughts, often with the sentence simply stopping there.
- も very often marks the second reason, since the reasons pile up in the same
  direction.
- In very polite speech し may follow long forms as well, the same way から can.
  日本が好きですし、日本語はおもしろいですし is fine in a formal register.

**Contrast**

- から presents a single reason as the reason. し presents reasons as items on a
  list and implies there could be more.
- ので (Lesson 12) is the softer, more objective causal connective and, like
  から, points at one cause rather than accumulating several.

### ～そうです (It looks like . . .)
<!-- id: g2-l13-sou-desu -->

**What it does.** Reports a guess about what something is like, made from
impressions rather than from conclusive evidence. You attach it to the base of
an adjective, and the result says the thing "seemingly" has that property.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | drop い, add そうです | おいしい → おいしそうです |
| いい (exception) | よさ + そうです | いい → よさそうです |
| な-adjective | drop な, add そうです | 元気(な) → 元気そうです |
| negative adjective | ない → なさ + そうです | 難しくない → 難しくなさそうです |
| before a noun | そうな + noun | 暖かそうなセーター |
| verb stem | stem + そうです, an event about to happen | 降る → 降りそうです |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| このケーキはおいしそうです。 | Kono keeki wa oishisou desu. | This cake looks delicious. |
| あの先生は厳しそうです。 | Ano sensei wa kibishisou desu. | That teacher looks strict. |
| この広告のアルバイトはよさそうです。 | Kono koukoku no arubaito wa yosasou desu. | The part-time job in this ad looks good. |
| おじいさんは幸せそうでした。 | Ojiisan wa shiawase sou deshita. | My grandfather looked happy. |
| このセーターは高くなさそうです。 | Kono seetaa wa takakunasasou desu. | This sweater does not look expensive. |
| 暖かそうな着物を着ていました。 | Atatakasou na kimono wo kite imashita. | She was wearing a warm-looking kimono. |
| 象が動きました。何か食べそうです。 | Zou ga ugokimashita. Nanika tabesou desu. | The elephant moved. It looks like it is about to eat something. |

**Notes**

- そう itself behaves as a な-adjective, which is why it takes そうな before a
  noun and そうでした in the past.
- The guesswork is usually visual, but that is not a requirement. そう is about
  lacking conclusive evidence. An apple is おいしそう until you taste it, and
  おいしい afterwards.
- Adjectives whose evidence is visual by nature will not take そう. きれいそう is
  wrong, because seeing something already settles whether it is pretty.
- The negative can sit on the adjective (難しくなさそうです) or on そう itself
  (難しそうじゃないです). Both are used.
- Attached to a verb stem it predicts an event rather than describing a
  property. 雨が降りそうです is a sky about to open.

**Contrast**

- The hearsay そうです introduced later attaches to plain forms, not to a stem or
  a bare adjective base. 雨が降るそうです is "I hear it will rain", while
  雨が降りそうです is "it looks like rain".

### ～てみる
<!-- id: g2-l13-temiru -->

**What it does.** Adds the sense of doing something tentatively, to see what
comes of it. You are not sure how it will turn out, so you go ahead and find
out.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| any verb | te-form + みる | 食べる → 食べてみる |
| conjugation | みる inflects as a regular ru-verb | 食べてみます, 食べてみた, 食べてみたい |
| writing | always in hiragana | 聞いてみる, not 聞いて見る |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 新しい店のケーキを食べてみます。 | Atarashii mise no keeki wo tabete mimasu. | I am going to try the cake at the new shop. |
| 分からない言葉は先生に聞いてみました。 | Wakaranai kotoba wa sensei ni kiite mimashita. | I asked the teacher about the words I did not know. |
| 空手を習ってみたいです。 | Karate wo naratte mitai desu. | I would like to give karate a try. |
| この募集の広告に電話してみます。 | Kono boshuu no koukoku ni denwa shite mimasu. | I will try calling about this job ad. |
| セーターを編んでみましたが、だめでした。 | Seetaa wo ande mimashita ga, dame deshita. | I had a go at knitting a sweater, but it came out badly. |
| 弁護士に相談してみたらどうですか。 | Bengoshi ni soudan shite mitara dou desu ka. | How about trying to talk it over with a lawyer? |

**Notes**

- The uncertainty concerns the outcome rather than your ability to perform the
  action. ～てみる does not carry any suggestion that you failed.
- みる comes from 見る and takes all the usual ru-verb endings, but as a helping
  verb it is written in kana.
- ～てみたい is extremely common for things you would like to sample once.

**Contrast**

- ～てみる is trying something out to see the result. Straining to accomplish
  something difficult is a different pattern, and 頑張る is the everyday word for
  that effort.

### なら
<!-- id: g2-l13-nara -->

**What it does.** Picks one item out and says the predicate holds for that item
in particular rather than across the board. The two effects are contrast, where the item is
set against something else, and limitation, where a smaller part is singled out
of a larger whole. なら always puts the item it marks in a positive light.

**Formation**

| pattern | shape | example |
|---|---|---|
| basic | noun A + なら + predicate X | ひらがなならわかります。 |
| particle kept | noun + に／で／から + なら | 大阪になら行ったことがあります。 |
| particle dropped | は, が, を never appear with なら | 空手ならできます。 |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| ゴルフはできませんが、空手ならできます。 | Gorufu wa dekimasen ga, karate nara dekimasu. | I cannot play golf, but karate I can do. |
| 外国語なら、韓国語が少し話せます。 | Gaikokugo nara, kankokugo ga sukoshi hanasemasu. | As for foreign languages, I can speak a little Korean. |
| 紅茶なら飲みますが、コーヒーは飲みません。 | Koucha nara nomimasu ga, koohii wa nomimasen. | Tea I will drink, but not coffee. |
| 三日ならバイクを貸せます。 | Mikka nara baiku wo kasemasu. | If it is for three days, I can lend you the motorbike. |
| ひらがなならぜんぶ読めます。 | Hiragana nara zenbu yomemasu. | If it is in hiragana, I can read all of it. |
| その店になら行ったことがあります。 | Sono mise ni nara itta koto ga arimasu. | To that shop I have been. |

**Notes**

- What なら marks is presented favourably, and whatever it is being contrasted
  with is left implied. 空手ならできます says karate is the one you have.
- に, で and から may stay in front of なら, and dropping them is equally fine. は,
  が and を never survive.
- なら is at home in answers, where it takes the topic the question raised and
  narrows it to the part you can actually speak to.
- Because it implies limits, なら can sound evasive if the questioner wanted a
  straight yes.

**Contrast**

- は marks a topic and can carry contrast, but it makes no claim that the
  statement is restricted to that item. なら does exactly that.

### 一週間に三回
<!-- id: g2-l13-frequency -->

**What it does.** States how often something happens over a stretch of time. The
period comes first, に marks it, and the frequency follows.

**Formation**

| pattern | shape | reads as |
|---|---|---|
| frequency over a period | (period) に (frequency) | (frequency) per (period) |

| period | romaji | typical frequency slot |
|---|---|---|
| 一日 | ichinichi | 一回, 二回, 三回 |
| 一週間 | isshuukan | 一回, 三回, 四回 |
| 一か月 | ikkagetsu | 一回, 二回 |
| 一年 | ichinen | 一回, 二回 |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 一日に二回歯を磨きます。 | Ichinichi ni nikai ha wo migakimasu. | I brush my teeth twice a day. |
| 一週間に四回アルバイトをしています。 | Isshuukan ni yonkai arubaito wo shite imasu. | I work part-time four times a week. |
| 一か月に一回おじいさんに電話します。 | Ikkagetsu ni ikkai ojiisan ni denwa shimasu. | I call my grandfather once a month. |
| 一年に二回国に帰ります。 | Ichinen ni nikai kuni ni kaerimasu. | I go back to my country twice a year. |
| 一日に十キロ走っています。 | Ichinichi ni jukkiro hashitte imasu. | I run ten kilometres a day. |
| 一週間に三回ゴルフの練習をします。 | Isshuukan ni sankai gorufu no renshuu wo shimasu. | I practise golf three times a week. |

**Notes**

- The order is the reverse of English. Japanese says the period first and the
  count second.
- The frequency slot is not limited to ～回. Distances and amounts fit there too,
  as in 一日に十キロ.
- ～回 triggers the usual sound changes. 一回 ikkai, 六回 rokkai, 八回 hakkai,
  十回 jukkai.
- Days counted with the native series (一日, 二日, 三日 . . . 十日) are read
  irregularly and have to be memorised as a set.

## Practice prompts

1. (g2-l13-potential) Give 12 dictionary-form verbs across all three classes and
   ask for the potential form, including both irregulars.
2. (g2-l13-potential) Produce 8 sentences about job skills where the object was
   originally marked with を, and show both the が version and the を version.
3. (g2-l13-potential) Write 6 sentences with できる whose subject is a する noun
   from this chapter, and flag 仕事をできる as the substandard variant.
4. (g2-l13-potential) Rewrite 5 potential sentences using ことができる and note the
   register shift.
5. (g2-l13-shi) Build 8 sentences with two し clauses each, mixing verbs,
   い-adjectives, な-adjectives and nouns so the だ rule gets exercised.
6. (g2-l13-shi) Give 5 sentences with a single し clause and state what unspoken
   extra reasons each one implies.
7. (g2-l13-sou-desu) Provide 10 adjectives, including いい and three negatives,
   and ask for the そうです form.
8. (g2-l13-sou-desu) Write 6 sentences describing people and food in a shop
   window, two of them using そうな before a noun.
9. (g2-l13-sou-desu) Present 5 adjectives and ask which of them cannot take そう,
   with a reason for each rejection.
10. (g2-l13-temiru) Write 8 sentences about trying something new during a job
    hunt, half in ～てみます and half in ～てみたいです.
11. (g2-l13-nara) Produce 6 question-and-answer pairs where the answer uses なら
    to narrow the question's topic, and mark which are contrast and which are
    limitation.
12. (g2-l13-nara) Give 5 nouns with particles attached and ask for both the
    particle-keeping and particle-dropping なら versions where allowed.
13. (g2-l13-frequency) Write 8 sentences about daily routines using each of 一日,
    一週間, 一か月 and 一年 at least once, with the counter sound changes correct.
14. (g2-l13-potential, g2-l13-shi, g2-l13-nara) Write a short job interview
    exchange in which the applicant states what they can do, gives two reasons
    for wanting the job, and narrows one claim with なら.
