# Genki II Lesson 22 (Education in Japan)

## Overview

Where Lesson 21 was about being on the receiving end of other people's actions,
this chapter is about authority over them. The causative supplies the core
grammar for making or letting somebody do something, and the same form plus a
giving-and-receiving verb narrows that ambiguity down to permission. なさい is
the command form that goes with the chapter's setting of parents, teachers and
cram schools. The remaining three points are unrelated to the causative and to
each other: ば is a conditional, のに expresses a complaint about how things
turned out, and のような／のように draws comparisons.

## Grammar

### Causative Sentences
<!-- id: g2-l22-causative -->

**What it does.** Says that one person caused another to perform an action.
Japanese uses a single form for both "made" and "let", and only context settles
which one is meant. The resulting verb conjugates as a ru-verb.

**Formation**

| verb class | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| ru-verb | drop る, add させる | 食べる → 食べさせる |
| u-verb | final -u sound → -a sound + せる | 行く → 行かせる, 読む → 読ませる |
| u-verb ending in う | う → わ + せる | 言う → 言わせる, 手伝う → 手伝わせる |
| する | replace with させる | 勉強する → 勉強させる |
| 来る | replace with こさせる | 来る → こさせる |

Two sentence patterns, chosen by the transitivity of the original verb:

| original verb | pattern | example |
|---|---|---|
| intransitive | X は Y を (causative) | 親は子供を塾に行かせます。 |
| transitive | X は Y に O を (causative) | 先生は学生に本を読ませました。 |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 先生は学生に日本語で話させました。 | Sensei wa gakusei ni nihongo de hanasasemashita. | The teacher made the students speak in Japanese. |
| 母は私に野菜を食べさせました。 | Haha wa watashi ni yasai wo tabesasemashita. | My mother made me eat vegetables. |
| 親は子供を塾に行かせます。 | Oya wa kodomo wo juku ni ikasemasu. | Parents send their children to cram school. |
| 部長は部下に書類を運ばせました。 | Buchou wa buka ni shorui wo hakobasemashita. | The manager had his subordinate carry the documents. |
| 先生は学生を走らせました。 | Sensei wa gakusei wo hashirasemashita. | The teacher made the students run. |

**Notes**

- With an intransitive verb the person made to act takes を. With a transitive
  verb they take に, because を is already spoken for by the direct object.
- Whether a sentence means "made" or "let" is genuinely ambiguous without
  context. The next grammar point is how Japanese resolves it.
- Using the causative towards a social superior is close to unthinkable. You do
  not 待たせる your professor on purpose, you apologise with お待たせしました.
- Causative forms are long. 手伝わせられました is grammatical, and Lesson 23 adds
  the causative-passive that produces it.

**Contrast**

- The passive from Lesson 21 makes the affected person the subject. The
  causative makes the instigator the subject. 読まれた is "it got read on me";
  読ませた is "I made someone read it".

### Causative + てあげる／てくれる／てもらう
<!-- id: g2-l22-causative-helping-verbs -->

**What it does.** Attaching a giving-and-receiving verb to a causative form
forces the "let" reading. The helping verb also marks who benefits and which
direction the favour flows, exactly as it does in Lesson 16.

**Formation**

| input | rule | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| causative te-form + あげる | you let someone else do it | I let them |
| causative te-form + くれる | someone let you do it | they let me |
| causative te-form + もらう | you got someone to let you do it | I had them let me |
| causative te-form + ください | asking for permission | please let me |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 母は私に留学させてくれました。 | Haha wa watashi ni ryuugaku sasete kuremashita. | My mother let me study abroad. |
| 課長に休ませてもらいました。 | Kachou ni yasumasete moraimashita. | I got my section chief to let me take time off. |
| 子供に好きなことをさせてあげました。 | Kodomo ni suki na koto wo sasete agemashita. | I let the child do what they wanted. |
| 私にやらせてください。 | Watashi ni yarasete kudasai. | Please let me do it. |
| この書類を翻訳させてください。 | Kono shorui wo honyaku sasete kudasai. | Please let me translate this document. |

**Notes**

- 〜させてください is the everyday way to volunteer for something, and it is much
  more common in speech than the bare causative.
- The polite escalation is 〜させていただけませんか, which combines this pattern
  with the humble いただく from Lesson 20.
- Without a helping verb, a bare causative aimed at yourself sounds like you are
  reporting being forced, so a request for permission needs one of these.

**Contrast**

- 〜てもいいですか (Lesson 6) asks whether an action is permitted in general.
  〜させてください asks a specific person to grant you permission, which is why it
  fits situations where somebody has authority over the outcome.

### Verb Stem + なさい
<!-- id: g2-l22-nasai -->

**What it does.** Forms a command issued downward, from a parent to a child, a
teacher to a student, or an exam paper to whoever is sitting it. It is firm but
not rough.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| any verb | ます-stem + なさい | 寝ます → 寝なさい |
| する verb | stem + しなさい | 予習します → 予習しなさい |
| 来る | き + なさい | 来ます → 来なさい |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 早く寝なさい。 | Hayaku nenasai. | Go to bed early. |
| よく予習しなさい。 | Yoku yoshuu shinasai. | Prepare your lessons properly. |
| 質問に答えなさい。 | Shitsumon ni kotaenasai. | Answer the question. |
| 部屋をきれいにしなさい。 | Heya wo kirei ni shinasai. | Tidy your room. |
| もう少しがんばりなさい。 | Mou sukoshi ganbarinasai. | Try a little harder. |

**Notes**

- Only usable towards someone clearly below you in the situation. Said to a
  peer it is patronising, said to a superior it is an insult.
- It appears constantly in written instructions, especially on tests, where the
  reader-writer relationship is exactly this asymmetric.
- There is no polite version. To soften a command you leave なさい behind and use
  〜てください or 〜ていただけませんか instead.

**Contrast**

- The plain imperative 寝ろ is harsher and reads as anger or as barked orders.
  なさい is the controlled version a parent uses.
- 〜てください is a request between equals and carries no authority claim.

### ～ば
<!-- id: g2-l22-ba -->

**What it does.** A conditional. The ば clause states what has to hold, and the
main clause states what follows from it. Genki introduces it alongside the fixed
phrase 〜ば大丈夫です, "as long as X, you will be fine".

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| u-verb | final -u sound → -e sound + ば | 行く → 行けば, 読む → 読めば |
| ru-verb | drop る, add れば | 食べる → 食べれば |
| する | replace with すれば | 復習する → 復習すれば |
| 来る | replace with くれば | 来る → くれば |
| い-adjective | い → ければ | 安い → 安ければ |
| negative | ない → なければ | 行かない → 行かなければ |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 練習すれば、上手になります。 | Renshuu sureba, jouzu ni narimasu. | If you practise, you will get good at it. |
| 早く行けば、間に合います。 | Hayaku ikeba, ma ni aimasu. | If you go early, you will be in time. |
| 安ければ、買います。 | Yasukereba, kaimasu. | If it is cheap, I will buy it. |
| 徹夜しなければ、大丈夫です。 | Tetsuya shinakereba, daijoubu desu. | As long as you do not stay up all night, you will be fine. |
| 毎日復習すれば、期末試験は心配ないです。 | Mainichi fukushuu sureba, kimatsu shiken wa shinpai nai desu. | If you review every day, the final exam is nothing to worry about. |

**Notes**

- When the ば clause is an affirmative action verb, something the listener
  controls, the main clause cannot be a command, request, invitation or
  statement of the speaker's intention. 東京に行けば、寿司を食べてください is wrong;
  use 行ったら instead.
- The restriction lifts when the ば clause is negative, an adjective, or a
  stative verb, which is why 安ければ買ってください and 分からなければ聞いてください are
  both fine.
- ば states a general condition rather than a sequence of events, so it suits
  advice and rules of thumb better than one-off plans.

**Contrast**

- 〜たら (Lesson 17) is the flexible everyday conditional and works for one-off
  situations and for "when" as well as "if". Where both fit, たら is safer.
- 〜と (Lesson 18) means the result follows automatically and inevitably, which
  makes it wrong for anything involving choice.

### ～のに
<!-- id: g2-l22-noni -->

**What it does.** Means "even though", and unlike a neutral "but" it carries the
speaker's dissatisfaction, surprise or complaint about the mismatch between the
two clauses.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | plain form + のに | 勉強したのに |
| い-adjective | plain form + のに | 高いのに |
| な-adjective | stem + な + のに | 静かなのに |
| noun | noun + な + のに | 日曜日なのに |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| たくさん勉強したのに、試験に失敗しました。 | Takusan benkyou shita noni, shiken ni shippai shimashita. | Even though I studied a lot, I failed the exam. |
| 高いのに、おいしくないです。 | Takai noni, oishikunai desu. | It is expensive and yet it does not taste good. |
| 日曜日なのに、働かなければいけません。 | Nichiyoubi na noni, hatarakanakereba ikemasen. | Even though it is Sunday, I have to work. |
| 約束したのに、来ませんでした。 | Yakusoku shita noni, kimasen deshita. | He promised and then he did not come. |
| 一人暮らしなのに、家事を全然しません。 | Hitorigurashi na noni, kaji wo zenzen shimasen. | He lives alone and yet he does no housework at all. |

**Notes**

- The emotional charge is part of the meaning, so のに is wrong for a neutral
  contrast where you are simply reporting two facts.
- A sentence can stop after the のに clause as a complaint left hanging.
  せっかく作ったのに… conveys the rest without saying it.
- な appears after both な-adjectives and nouns, which is the usual slip.

**Contrast**

- 〜けど and 〜が are the neutral contrastive connectives. Swapping のに for けど
  drains the sentence of the speaker's irritation.
- 〜ても means "even if" and points at a hypothetical, where のに points at
  something that actually happened.

### ～のような／～のように
<!-- id: g2-l22-noyouni -->

**What it does.** Draws a comparison to a named thing or person. のような
modifies a noun, のように modifies a verb or an adjective.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| noun modifying a noun | noun + のような + noun | 夢のような話 |
| noun modifying a verb | noun + のように + verb | 猿のように登る |
| noun modifying an adjective | noun + のように + adjective | 雪のように白い |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 田中さんのような人になりたいです。 | Tanaka-san no you na hito ni naritai desu. | I want to become a person like Mr Tanaka. |
| 彼は日本人のように話します。 | Kare wa nihonjin no you ni hanashimasu. | He speaks like a Japanese person. |
| 猿のように木に登ります。 | Saru no you ni ki ni noborimasu. | He climbs trees like a monkey. |
| 夢のような話です。 | Yume no you na hanashi desu. | It is a story like something out of a dream. |
| 先輩のようにペラペラになりたいです。 | Senpai no you ni perapera ni naritai desu. | I want to become fluent like my senior. |

**Notes**

- The split is the ordinary な versus に adjective ending, since ようだ behaves as
  a な-adjective.
- みたいな and みたいに are the casual equivalents and attach directly to the noun
  with no の.
- 〜のように is also used for instructions in the sense of "as shown", as in
  この写真のように, which is a different flavour of the same pattern.

**Contrast**

- ～そうです (Lesson 17) reports appearance or hearsay rather than similarity.
- 〜らしい claims something is typical of its category, so 男らしい is "manly"
  while 男のよう is "resembling a man".

   〜させてください, then escalate each to 〜させていただけませんか.
5. (g2-l22-nasai) Give 8 instructions a parent or teacher would issue, and for
   each state who could not be addressed this way.
6. (g2-l22-ba) Provide 10 verbs and adjectives and ask for the ば form.

   against volitional main clauses, and ask which are wrong and why.
8. (g2-l22-noni) Write 6 のに sentences where the speaker is visibly annoyed,
   then rewrite each with けど and describe what is lost.
9. (g2-l22-noyouni) Build 8 comparisons, four modifying nouns and four
   modifying verbs or adjectives, using this chapter's animal and person nouns.
10. (g2-l22-causative, g2-l22-nasai, g2-l22-noni) Write a short monologue by a
    student at a cram school that uses all three patterns.
