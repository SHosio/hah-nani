# Genki II Lesson 22 (Education in Japan)

## Overview

Where Lesson 21 was about being on the receiving end of other people's actions,
this chapter is about authority over them. The causative supplies the core
grammar for making or letting somebody do something, and the same form plus a
giving-and-receiving verb narrows that ambiguity down to permission in almost
all cases. なさい is the command form that goes with the chapter's setting of
parents, teachers and cram schools. The remaining three points are unrelated to
the causative and to each other. ば is a conditional, のに joins two facts that
clash with expectation, and のような／のように draws comparisons.

## Grammar

### Causative Sentences
<!-- id: g2-l22-causative -->

**What it does.** Says that one person caused another to perform an action.
Japanese uses a single form for both "made" and "let", and only context settles
which one is meant. The resulting verb conjugates as a ru-verb.

**Formation**

| verb class | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| ru-verb | drop る, add させる | 食べる → 食べさせる |
| u-verb | final -u sound → -a sound + せる | 行く → 行かせる, 読む → 読ませる |
| u-verb ending in う | う → わ + せる | 言う → 言わせる, 手伝う → 手伝わせる |
| する | replace with させる | 勉強する → 勉強させる |
| 来る | replace with こさせる | 来る → こさせる |

One sentence pattern carries the whole point. The running example below is
先生は学生に毎日漢字を練習させます。

| slot | marking | in the example |
|---|---|---|
| director, who decides what is allowed and what gets done | は or が | 先生は |
| cast, who performs the action | usually に | 学生に |
| action | causative verb | 毎日漢字を練習させます |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 先生は学生に日本語で話させました。 | Sensei wa gakusei ni nihongo de hanasasemashita. | The teacher made the students speak in Japanese. |
| 母は私に野菜を食べさせました。 | Haha wa watashi ni yasai wo tabesasemashita. | My mother made me eat vegetables. |
| 先生は学生に走らせました。 | Sensei wa gakusei ni hashirasemashita. | The teacher had the students run. |
| 先輩は後輩に計画を立てさせました。 | Senpai wa kouhai ni keikaku wo tatesasemashita. | The senior had the juniors draw up the plan. |
| 親は子供を塾に行かせます。 | Oya wa kodomo wo juku ni ikasemasu. | The parents make their child go to cram school. |

**Notes**

- The cast takes に by default, whatever the original verb was. を appears
  instead in two situations. One is a caused action that is a reflex, such as
  crying or laughing, 弟を泣かせてしまいました. The other is a verb that has no
  direct object of its own, which leaves を free for the cast, so
  親は子供を塾に行かせます works and with 行く and 座る the particle is genuinely up
  for grabs. A verb that already claims を blocks this, which is why
  × 両親は私を本を読ませました fails on two を in one clause.
- Whether a sentence means "made" or "let" is genuinely ambiguous without
  context. When the helping verb てあげる, てくれる or てもらう follows a causative
  verb, you can assume in almost all cases that it is a "let" causative. Those
  three helping verbs are Lesson 16. The book presents this as part of the
  causative rather than as a separate point:

  | pattern | meaning |
  |---|---|
  | causative te-form + あげる／くれる／もらう | (director) *lets* (cast) do it, in almost all cases |
  | causative te-form + ください | please let me do it |

  母は私に留学させてくれました (Haha wa watashi ni ryuugaku sasete kuremashita) is
  "my mother let me study abroad".
- 〜させてください is the everyday way to volunteer for something or to ask
  permission, and is far more common in speech than a bare causative.
  私に翻訳させてください (Watashi ni honyaku sasete kudasai) is "please let me do the
  translation". The polite escalation is 〜させていただけませんか, built on the
  〜ていただけませんか of Lesson 16.
- Using the causative towards a social superior is close to unthinkable. You do
  not 待たせる your professor on purpose, you apologise with お待たせしました.
- Causative forms are long. 手伝わせられました is grammatical, and Lesson 23 adds
  the causative-passive that produces it.

**Contrast**

- The passive from Lesson 21 makes the affected person the subject. The
  causative makes the instigator the subject. 読まれた is "it got read on me";
  読ませた is "I made someone read it".

### Verb Stem + なさい
<!-- id: g2-l22-nasai -->

**What it does.** Forms a command issued downward, from a parent to a child, a
teacher to a student, or an exam paper to whoever is sitting it. It is firm but
not rough.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| any verb | ます-stem + なさい | 寝ます → 寝なさい |
| する verb | stem + しなさい | 予習します → 予習しなさい |
| 来る | き + なさい | 来ます → 来なさい |
| negative "don't do . . ." | dictionary form + のを + やめなさい | ゲームをする → ゲームをするのをやめなさい |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 早く寝なさい。 | Hayaku nenasai. | Go to bed early. |
| よく予習しなさい。 | Yoku yoshuu shinasai. | Prepare your lessons properly. |
| 質問に答えなさい。 | Shitsumon ni kotaenasai. | Answer the question. |
| 部屋をきれいにしなさい。 | Heya wo kirei ni shinasai. | Tidy your room. |
| もう少しがんばりなさい。 | Mou sukoshi ganbarinasai. | Try a little harder. |
| ゲームをするのをやめなさい。 | Geemu wo suru no wo yamenasai. | Stop playing games. |

**Notes**

- Only usable towards someone clearly below you in the situation. Said to a
  peer it is patronising, said to a superior it is an insult.
- It appears constantly in written instructions, especially on tests, where the
  reader-writer relationship is exactly this asymmetric.
- There is no negative なさい form. You express "don't do . . ." with the
  dictionary form plus のを and finish with やめなさい, from the verb やめる.
- なさい has no gentler variant that is still this command. To soften you leave
  なさい behind and use 〜てください or 〜ていただけませんか (Lesson 16) instead. Beyond
  this lesson, the honorific なさる it descends from also yields なさいませ, which
  belongs to shop and hotel service language rather than to this pattern.

**Contrast**

- The plain imperative 寝ろ is harsher and reads as anger or as barked orders.
  なさい is the controlled version a parent uses.
- 〜てください is a request between equals and carries no authority claim.

### ～ば
<!-- id: g2-l22-ba -->

**What it does.** A conditional. The ば clause states the condition, provided
that the consequence in the main clause will follow. Lesson 18's ばよかった was
the first ば-form this course used, and this is where the form itself gets laid
out.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| u-verb | final -u sound → -e sound + ば | 行く → 行けば, 読む → 読めば |
| ru-verb | drop る, add れば | 食べる → 食べれば |
| する | replace with すれば | 復習する → 復習すれば |
| 来る | replace with くれば | 来る → くれば |
| い-adjective | い → ければ | 安い → 安ければ |
| negative | ない → なければ | 行かない → 行かなければ |
| な-adjective | だ → なら (Lesson 13) or であれば | 静かだ → 静かなら／静かであれば |
| noun | だ → なら (Lesson 13) or であれば | 先生だ → 先生なら／先生であれば |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 練習すれば、上手になります。 | Renshuu sureba, jouzu ni narimasu. | If you practise, you will get good at it. |
| 早く行けば、間に合います。 | Hayaku ikeba, ma ni aimasu. | If you go early, you will be in time. |
| 安ければ、買います。 | Yasukereba, kaimasu. | If it is cheap, I will buy it. |
| 徹夜しなければ、大丈夫です。 | Tetsuya shinakereba, daijoubu desu. | As long as you do not stay up all night, you will be fine. |
| 毎日復習すれば、期末試験は心配ないです。 | Mainichi fukushuu sureba, kimatsu shiken wa shinpai nai desu. | If you review every day, the final exam is nothing to worry about. |
| 分からなければ、先輩に聞けばいいんです。 | Wakaranakereba, senpai ni kikeba ii n desu. | If you do not understand, all you have to do is ask your senior. |

**Notes**

- You normally use A ば B when the condition A guarantees a *good result* in B.
  早く行けば、間に合います is natural, while ?? 徹夜すれば、試験に失敗します sounds odd,
  because B is a bad outcome. It is not ungrammatical, it just wants another
  conditional, and 徹夜したら、試験に失敗します says it properly.
- The oddness is about implication rather than about grammar, so embedding the
  same clause in a larger sentence that cancels the good-result reading rescues
  it. 徹夜すれば試験に失敗するのはわかっていました is fine.
- That good-result implication is why A ば B is so often advice, with B reduced
  to a vacuous generic expression like 大丈夫です or いいんです. 早く寝れば大丈夫です
  and 毎日復習すればいいんです both amount to "that is all you need to do".
- ば states a general condition rather than a sequence of events, so it suits
  advice and rules of thumb better than one-off plans.
- Beyond this lesson, ば carries a further restriction Genki does not mention.
  When the ば clause is an affirmative action verb, something the listener
  controls, the main clause cannot be a command, request, invitation or
  statement of the speaker's intention. 東京に行けば、寿司を食べてください is wrong,
  and 行ったら fixes it. The restriction lifts when the ば clause is negative, an
  adjective or a stative verb, which is why 安ければ買ってください and
  分からなければ聞いてください are both fine.

**Contrast**

- 〜たら (Lesson 17) is the flexible everyday conditional and works for one-off
  situations and for "when" as well as "if". Where both fit, たら is safer.
- 〜と (Lesson 18) means the result follows automatically and inevitably, which
  makes it wrong for anything involving choice.

### ～のに
<!-- id: g2-l22-noni -->

**What it does.** Connects two facts A and B that both hold in spite of the
expectation that if A is the case, B is not. A のに B is therefore "A, but
contrary to expectations, B too", or "B, despite the fact A".

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| verb | plain form + のに | 勉強したのに |
| い-adjective | plain form + のに | 高いのに |
| な-adjective | stem + な + のに | 静かなのに |
| noun | noun + な + のに | 日曜日なのに |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| たくさん勉強したのに、試験に失敗しました。 | Takusan benkyou shita noni, shiken ni shippai shimashita. | Even though I studied a lot, I failed the exam. |
| 高いのに、おいしくないです。 | Takai noni, oishikunai desu. | It is expensive and yet it does not taste good. |
| 日曜日なのに、働かなければいけません。 | Nichiyoubi na noni, hatarakanakereba ikemasen. | Even though it is Sunday, I have to work. |
| 約束したのに、来ませんでした。 | Yakusoku shita noni, kimasen deshita. | He promised and then he did not come. |
| 一人暮らしなのに、家事を全然しません。 | Hitorigurashi na noni, kaji wo zenzen shimasen. | He lives alone and yet he does no housework at all. |

**Notes**

- Because のに connects two facts, the B clause has to be factual too. Requests,
  invitations and suggestions do not fit there:

  | wrong | compare |
  |---|---|
  | × 部屋が少しせまいのに、ここで待ってください。 | 部屋が少しせまいですが、ここで待ってください。 |
  | × あまり安くないのに、これを買いましょう。 | あまり安くないけど、これを買いましょう。 |

  The repair is the same both times. Swap のに for a plain contrastive が or けど.
- な appears after both な-adjectives and nouns, which is the usual slip. It is
  the same な the explanatory んです takes.
- Beyond this lesson, のに often carries the speaker's dissatisfaction or
  surprise on top of the contrast, which is why a sentence can stop after the
  のに clause as a complaint left hanging. せっかく作ったのに… conveys the rest
  without saying it.

**Contrast**

- 〜けど and 〜が are the neutral contrastive connectives, and they are what you
  fall back on whenever the B clause is a request or a suggestion.
- 〜ても means "even if" and points at a hypothetical, where のに points at
  something that actually happened.

### ～のような／～のように
<!-- id: g2-l22-noyouni -->

**What it does.** Draws a comparison to a named thing or person. A のような B is
"a B like A", and it has two readings. Either B has the same quality or
appearance as A, or A is one example of B. のような modifies a noun, のように
modifies a verb or an adjective and describes an action done in the same way as
A or a characteristic comparable to A.

**Formation**

| input | rule | example |
|---|---|---|
| noun modifying a noun, shared quality | noun + のような + noun | 夢のような話 |
| noun modifying a noun, A is an example of B | noun + のような + noun | 塾のような場所 |
| noun modifying a verb | noun + のように + verb | 猿のように登る |
| noun modifying an adjective | noun + のように + adjective | 雪のように白い |

**Examples**

| jp | romaji | en |
|---|---|---|
| 私は先輩のようなやさしい人と働きたいです。 | Watashi wa senpai no you na yasashii hito to hatarakitai desu. | I want to work with a kind person like my senior. |
| 塾のような場所は日本にたくさんあります。 | Juku no you na basho wa Nihon ni takusan arimasu. | There are many places such as cram schools in Japan. |
| 彼は日本人のように話します。 | Kare wa nihonjin no you ni hanashimasu. | He speaks like a Japanese person. |
| 猿のように木に登ります。 | Saru no you ni ki ni noborimasu. | He climbs trees like a monkey. |
| 夢のような話です。 | Yume no you na hanashi desu. | It is a story like something out of a dream. |
| 先輩のようにペラペラになりたいです。 | Senpai no you ni perapera ni naritai desu. | I want to become fluent like my senior. |

**Notes**

- The split is the ordinary な versus に adjective ending, since ようだ behaves as
  a な-adjective.
- Which of the two readings of のような is meant comes from context. 夢のような話
  is a story with the quality of a dream, while 塾のような場所 is places of which
  a cram school is one instance.
- みたいな and みたいに (Lesson 17) are the casual equivalents and attach directly
  to the noun with no の.
- 〜のように is also used for instructions in the sense of "as shown", as in
  この写真のように, which is a different flavour of the same pattern.

**Contrast**

- ～そうです (Lesson 17) reports appearance or hearsay rather than similarity.
- 〜らしい claims something is typical of its category, so 男らしい is "manly"
  while 男のよう is "resembling a man".
